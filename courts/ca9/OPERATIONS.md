## Court and jurisdiction
29 authorized positions; Alaska, Arizona, California (four districts), Hawaii, Idaho, Montana, Nevada, Oregon, Washington (two districts), Guam and Northern Mariana Islands. 15 districts total. Distinguish appeals from final judgments, authorized interlocutory appeals, agency petitions, Tax Court, bankruptcy and writs; each needs its own statutory basis, timeliness and review standard. No factfinding trial at appellate level. FRAP 10 and Circuit 30-1 govern record/excerpts; distinguish record correction from new evidence. FRAP 28 and Circuit 28-2 structure briefs; check issue preservation, jurisdiction, standards of review, record pinpoints and relief. Filing under Circuit 25-5 / ACMS as applicable. Check sealing and redaction (27-13). No actual private records in public repo.

## Calendar and panel — GO 3.2, 3.3
Ordinary oral argument panel: three distinct judges; at least two Ninth Circuit members and at least one active Ninth judge at drawing. Chief judge presides if present; otherwise most senior active Ninth judge. Clerk's assignment accounts for workload, locations, conflicts and random selection. Engine checks composition and presiding judge at initial assignment; automatic calendar randomization, exceptions, recusal substitution and visiting judge designation procedures are not implemented. For fictional records, use verified judicial status and seniority rank; numbers are simulation data, not real judicial rank claims. Conflict check and source-backed jurisdiction required before registration.

## Argument and conference
FRAP 34(a)(2): no argument only with all three judges' recorded assent after record/brief review and permissible reason (frivolity, controlling resolution or no material aid to decision). Otherwise schedule argument. Appellant opens and concludes; questions are unrestricted within lawful argument. GO 4.2: if significant unbriefed issue may decide the case, consider further briefing/argument before decision. No separate factual investigation or unauthorized ex parte contact. Conference is private. Simulation requires independent initial votes, a recorded disposition and author assignment before drafting; majority author chosen from majority coalition as simulation convention, not claim of exact real internal assignment. No forced agreement. GO 4.4: offer specific replacement language on revision requests.

## Opinions — GO 4.3–4.5; Circuit 36-1–36-3
Use OPINION, MEMORANDUM or ORDER. Published opinion criteria include development/clarification of federal law, neglected doctrine, criticism, unusual/publicly significant issue, published lower decision subject to exception, or separate expression with publication request. Memorandum is ordinarily nonprecedential (exceptions for preclusion/law of case). Separate concurrence/dissent is not binding by itself. Distinguish judgment majority from agreement on reasoning; join by part, not automatic across versions. Ordinary written disposition needs majority agreement; GO 4.5 has special delayed separate-expression process (60 days plus 10 days subject to extensions); v1 requires all panel positions and blocks unsupported/exception cases instead of auto-applying deadlines. Record certification, draft revision and publication review. Issuing opinion is not mandate or closure.

## Rehearing and mandate — documented, not executable yet
FRAP 40 now governs both panel and en banc rehearing; Circuit Rules 40-2/40-3 and GO 5 apply. Eligible active judges vote on whether to rehear; ordinary limited en banc court is chief plus ten drawn by lot (replacement/eligibility exceptions, including certain senior judges). Full court rehearing possible. GO 5.7 en banc draft schedule is distinct from ordinary panel. Grant ordinarily vacates panel opinion subject to reinstatement. FRAP 41 and GO 4.6 govern mandate, stay/recall and exceptional immediate issuance. Case-specific timing must be verified; no automatic mandate/appeal deadline calculator. Do not simulate en banc or special panel as ordinary three-judge case.
