# ORDER ON DEFENDANT'S MOTION FOR SUMMARY JUDGMENT

STATE OF MAINE · SUPERIOR COURT · CUMBERLAND, ss. · CV-2025-418

HARBORLIGHT COLD STORAGE, LLC v. PENOBSCOT POWER SYSTEMS, INC.

June 3, 2026 · Adrian Webb, Justice of the Superior Court (fictional)

## I. Motion and record

Defendant moves for judgment on the single contract count. Plaintiff opposes. The parties submitted the motion on the written record without an evidentiary hearing. The Court has considered the written agreement, the numbered factual submissions and responses, the submitted affidavits and their attachments, and the parties' memoranda.

For purposes of this motion, the following core events are not genuinely disputed: a utility interruption began on November 14, 2024 at 05:40; a qualifying notice reached Defendant at 09:55; an initial mobilization was paused following a closure report; and temporary generation was operating at the facility at 07:20 on November 16. The parties also agree that the agreement contains the baseline response, external-delay excuse, reasonable-efforts sentence and safety provision reproduced in the record. [R-003 §§3.1–3.3; R-011].

The record does not establish by agreement that every possible route remained closed throughout the entire delay. The parties dispute the significance of the conditional detour, the Belfast unit, the provisional reservation and the timing of further inquiry. The Court does not determine witness credibility or adopt an engineering reconstruction of a proposed alternate trip.

## II. Contractual disposition

Section 3.1's twelve-hour undertaking is expressly subject to section 3.2. The first sentence of section 3.2 excuses responsibility for delay caused by severe weather, road closure or conditions beyond Defendant's reasonable control. The undisputed initial obstacle falls within that language.

The Court construes the second sentence's commercially reasonable-efforts direction as an operational aspiration within the stated excuse rather than an independent source of damages liability for a severe-weather delay. On that construction, the retainer did not transfer the relevant weather-delay risk back to Provider whenever a customer could later propose additional investigation. Section 3.3's safety protection reinforces the limited nature of the response undertaking.

Plaintiff urges that this reading deprives the second sentence of legal effect. The Court disagrees. In the Court's view, the sentence informs operational performance without displacing the express allocation of responsibility for the delay at issue. The Court regards the agreement as unambiguous on that point and does not resolve an ambiguity by choosing between competing witness accounts.

Accordingly, the parties' disagreements concerning later route verification and resource inquiry do not constitute material factual disputes under the construction the Court adopts. This ruling does not find that no alternate inquiry was possible. It holds that the asserted failure to make such an inquiry does not create the damages liability alleged under this weather-delay provision.

## III. Matters not decided

The initial loss material raises substantial questions concerning the point at which inventory became unmerchantable, the proper valuation and the extent of expense attributable to delayed performance. The Court does not reach those questions as independent grounds for judgment. It neither fixes the amount of loss nor determines that all inventory would have been lost irrespective of any later conduct.

The Court likewise makes no determination concerning an actual carrier's availability, a particular rig's weight clearance or the factual effect of a hypothetical earlier connection. Those matters are unnecessary under the contractual interpretation that resolves the motion.

## IV. Entry

Defendant's motion is GRANTED. Final judgment shall enter for Penobscot Power Systems, Inc. on Count I, the only count. Harborlight shall take nothing on its complaint. No claims or parties remain for adjudication. Costs remain subject to the applicable procedure.
