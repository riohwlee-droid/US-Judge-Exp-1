# DEFENDANT'S MOTION FOR SUMMARY JUDGMENT AND SUPPORTING MEMORANDUM

STATE OF MAINE · SUPERIOR COURT · CUMBERLAND, ss. · CV-2025-418

HARBORLIGHT COLD STORAGE, LLC, Plaintiff

v. PENOBSCOT POWER SYSTEMS, INC., Defendant

Defendant Penobscot Power Systems, Inc. moves pursuant to M.R. Civ. P. 56 for judgment on Count I of the complaint. The motion is supported by the separately filed statement of material facts, the agreement and the affidavit of Owen Sutherland. Defendant does not request a factual finding that every road remained closed or a declaration fixing the amount of Harborlight's loss.

## I. The undertaking the parties actually purchased

The standby retainer purchased a response arrangement subject to defined conditions. Section 3.1 supplies the twelve-hour undertaking but expressly makes that undertaking subject to section 3.2. The first sentence of section 3.2 addresses delay caused by severe weather and road closure. Section 3.3 protects personnel and prohibits transportation in violation of closure orders. [R-003 §§1–3]. Reading the retainer as an unconditional promise to keep refrigeration operating through a regional storm would disregard those limitations.

The facts material to the asserted exclusion are not reasonably disputed: utility power was lost during severe weather, the qualifying request was received at 09:55, the initial transport was paused after a closure report, and generation was not operating until November 16. The parties' disagreement concerns the significance of resource and route information obtained after the initial obstruction. [R-006 §§2–10; R-007].

## II. The reasonable-efforts language does not make Provider an insurer

Harborlight relies on the second sentence of section 3.2. Defendant recognizes that sentence is part of the contract. The issue is what work it performs within the stated excuse. The phrase commercially reasonable efforts does not guarantee a successful result. The words available and when practicable require attention to actual resource constraints, not merely a nominal equipment rating or a general notice that some commercial traffic may proceed.

The Belfast notation does not supply a crew, release the provisional reservation or establish that a lawful transport configuration could reach this site. The road bulletin requires individual checks. A notation that an alternate route exists for qualifying vehicles therefore does not show that the Provider was capable of completing a safe hookup within the period on which the claim depends. [R-006 §§4–8,13; R-007; R-008 §C].

Defendant contends that the reasonable-efforts sentence guides operations within the excuse and cannot be read to transfer the consequences of every weather-caused interruption back to Provider. Alternatively, even if a failure of reasonable efforts can support a claim in some circumstances, the claimant must identify evidence connecting the alleged failure to an available and practicable response.

## III. The submitted loss material does not establish avoided loss

Frost identifies a temperature-target excursion, but she does not determine when each lot became unmerchantable. The $91,400 is a book-value figure, and the response-expense category includes costs that could have arisen even with earlier backup. [R-004 §§11–16; R-009]. Defendant disputes whether the gap between the actual and asserted earlier response caused a compensable amount.

Defendant does not ask the Court to find that all inventory was already lost at 16:15 on November 14. Its position is that a general temperature excursion and aggregate accounting figure cannot simply be substituted for proof of the causal connection alleged. Should the Court deny the motion on the contractual issue, Defendant reserves its causation and valuation defenses for further proceedings.

## IV. Requested ruling

Defendant requests judgment on Count I under its interpretation of the excuse and reasonable-efforts provisions and on the submitted record. It does not request an advisory ruling concerning every possible application of such a provision. Any ruling should identify the contractual ground on which the motion is resolved so that the parties can understand which factual disputes the Court regards as material.
