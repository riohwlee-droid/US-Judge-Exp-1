## STATEMENT CONCERNING APPELLANT'S PRESENTATION

Provider accepts the core chronology but disagrees with the inference Appellant seeks to draw from it. A dispatch system may show a machine in a yard, a provisional commitment to another customer and a regional road-information item without showing a legally and operationally available delivery. The agreement concerns temporary equipment that must be selected, transported and safely connected. It does not promise the ability to move equipment through every interval in which some commercial vehicle may use a detour.

Section 3.1 is expressly subject to section 3.2. Section 3.2 addresses causes outside Provider's reasonable control, and section 3.3 protects personnel and legal compliance. [R-003 §§3.1–3.3]. These provisions were part of the same bargain as the response undertaking. Appellant's description of a twelve-hour baseline cannot be detached from them.

There is no dispute that the storm affected the initial transport and that no permanent backup generator had been purchased for the facility. There is also no disputed admission that the Belfast unit could have been delivered that evening. Sutherland distinguishes readiness from deployability and identifies a provisional reservation. Frost disclaims knowledge of the Provider's internal allocation and of a particular rig's ability to use the detour. [R-004 §§6,8; R-006 §§4–7,13].

Provider does not assert that every road remained closed until energization. It does not convert the 16:15 bulletin into such a statement. Its point is narrower: a notice conditionally permitting qualifying traffic, and requiring individual checks, does not establish that this proposed movement was practicable. [R-008 §C]. The difference is essential to understanding its defense.

## QUESTION AND STANDARD

Whether the agreement and the submitted record permit judgment for Provider when the initial response was obstructed by severe weather and the nonmovant identified no confirmed transport configuration or causal avoided-loss account supporting its proposed alternative response.

Provider agrees that the grant of summary judgment and the legal interpretation issues are reviewed de novo. It also agrees that the Law Court should not conduct a new evidentiary hearing or decide a disputed witness's credibility from material not presented below. The question is what the submitted documents support under the parties' actual undertaking.

## ARGUMENT I. REASONABLE EFFORTS MUST BE READ WITH THE EXPRESS LIMITATIONS

### A. Provider's position does not depend on deleting the second sentence

Appellant portrays the defense as if it claimed the reasonable-efforts language can never matter. Provider's position is that its content is bounded by the stated resources, circumstances, safety restriction and practicability qualification. A duty of reasonable efforts is not the same as a promise that some alternative can always be located or that delayed performance after a storm establishes an actionable failure.

The retainer did not dedicate a particular generator to Harborlight. The agreement permits use of Provider equipment or other suitable sources without specifying that Harborlight's demand displaces every other customer commitment. [R-003 §§1.3,3.2]. The provisional reservation may not prove an absolute obstacle, but neither is it irrelevant merely because the machine remained at Belfast. Allocation, transport and hookup are different stages of mobilization.

The agreement's recognition of alternate equipment also does not establish a source of that equipment. A party cannot fill a gap in proof about availability by pointing to language that contemplates alternatives when practicable. The qualifier is part of the provision Appellant seeks to enforce.

### B. The safety limitation is not merely a defense to an order to break the law

Harborlight emphasizes that it did not request illegal driving. Provider accepts that description of the claim. Yet uncertainty about route restrictions, staffing and safe connections can bear on commercially reasonable choices before a crew moves. The safety clause would be diminished if every cautious deferral were treated as an inference of breach solely because some additional inquiry might conceivably have been made.

Sutherland states that he regarded an unconfirmed route differently from a verified dispatch path and believed labor and escort resources would be difficult to obtain. [R-006 §§6–8]. His affidavit is not a road certification and does not purport to be one. Appellant's alternative must nevertheless remain tied to what the available record establishes, rather than to an operational plan built in the appellate brief.

### C. The interpretive cases do not decide this allocation

Keep and Dahlem provide the applicable legal framework. Neither supplies a holding that this particular sentence is an independent source of damages in this factual setting. Appellant's analysis of the words must therefore be assessed with the remainder of the undertaking and the stated limits, not treated as a direct application of a generator case that does not exist among the cited authorities.

Provider acknowledges that the lower court used the phrase operational aspiration. If the Court regards that phrase as too broad, it should distinguish the lower court's terminology from the question whether the actual motion record supports the judgment. The Provider has not requested a rule excusing deliberate indifference whenever any storm occurs.

## ARGUMENT II. THE IDENTIFIED RECORD DOES NOT CONFIRM THE PROPOSED ALTERNATIVE

### A. The resource entry leaves material operational questions unanswered

The Belfast entry identifies rating, readiness and a provisional reservation. It does not provide vehicle weight, connection details, a driver assignment or the release of the competing reservation. [R-007]. A unit whose nominal output exceeds 180 kW is not thereby shown to be available for every site and every transport configuration. The absence of a dedicated-equipment obligation reinforces the need to address these distinctions.

Appellant fairly acknowledges the limits of the notation. The consequence is that the notation alone cannot prove the alternate performance around which its argument is organized. Provider does not ask the Court to invent the opposite facts. It asks the Court to consider the limits of the evidence actually proffered.

### B. The bulletin is conditional rather than a shipment clearance

The bulletin expressly leaves individual bridge and condition verification to drivers. There is no submitted map or numerical limit for the proposed rig, and Frost did not drive or measure the route. [R-004 §6; R-008 §C]. Provider's log records receipt of the information, not a completed clearance.

Harborlight emphasizes the interval before Sutherland's next-day confirmation. That interval may invite questions. It does not, by itself, answer them. The absence of a named carrier refusal in the affidavits cannot be converted into sworn testimony from a willing carrier. Likewise, the absence of an inquiry entry is not an affirmative record that no employee considered any operational question.

The parties' separate statements preserve those distinctions. [R-011 B.11–13, C.7–8, D.7–8]. Provider asks the Court to consider the actual qualifications rather than treating an admitted document entry as an admission of Appellant's ultimate inference.

### C. An inquiry-based claim still requires a connection to the asserted loss

Appellant now frames the claim partly as a failure to investigate. That formulation cannot eliminate the need to connect the claimed failure to the loss for which compensation is sought. It is not enough to show that a phone call could have occurred if no available evidence connects the additional inquiry to an attainable response affecting the claimed inventory or expense.

Provider does not ask for a requirement that every alternative be proved with certainty at this stage. It asks that the claim be evaluated using the undertaking, the submitted facts and the evidentiary limits admitted by Appellant's own witnesses. A remand should not be described as an appellate finding that the contemplated inquiry would have produced a successful dispatch.

## ARGUMENT III. THE DAMAGES MATERIAL AND THE PROPER DISPOSITION

The loss summary separates two categories and does not establish the condition of each lot at a particular time. Frost cannot determine when each item became unmerchantable. She acknowledges that some response expense could have arisen anyway. Bell identifies the claim total without offering an independent spoilage opinion. [R-004 §§13–16; R-005 §§6–8; R-009].

Provider recognizes that the Superior Court expressly declined to make causation an independent ground. [R-013 §III]. It does not present that observation as a finding after trial. Its alternative argument is that the deficiencies remain relevant to the scope of any appellate ruling and to any further proceedings. If the Court does not affirm, it should leave those defenses intact.

If the Court vacates because it rejects the trial court's construction, it should not resolve Provider's efforts, staffing, alternative equipment or causation adversely to it. There has been no trial, no credibility determination and no expert record establishing the asserted earlier preservation of inventory. Reversal of a categorical legal ruling would not resolve those separate questions.

Provider requests affirmance. Alternatively, it requests a remand limited by the legal interpretation actually adopted, with no appellate finding of breach, feasible alternate delivery or compensable loss and no judgment entered for Harborlight.

## CONCLUSION

Affirm the final judgment. If the Court determines that the reasonable-efforts provision requires further proceedings, remand without factual findings establishing liability or damages and without treating the conditional bulletin or resource entry as a conclusive operational plan.
