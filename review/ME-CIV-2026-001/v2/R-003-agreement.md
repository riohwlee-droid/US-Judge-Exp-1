# EMERGENCY GENERATION SUPPORT AGREEMENT

Agreement No. EGSA-HCS-240517

This Emergency Generation Support Agreement is entered into as of May 17, 2024, by Harborlight Cold Storage, LLC, a Maine limited liability company (Customer), and Penobscot Power Systems, Inc., a Maine corporation (Provider). The Service Site and operational contacts are identified in Schedule A. The parties agree as follows.

## 1. Covered service and standby retainer

1.1 Provider will maintain a dispatch plan for temporary generation capable of supporting the refrigeration load identified in Schedule A. The undertaking concerns emergency mobilization and hookup of mobile equipment. It does not include ownership, maintenance or operation of the electric utility's transmission or distribution facilities.

1.2 Customer will pay an annual standby retainer of $45,600, in twelve monthly installments of $3,800. The first coverage period begins June 1, 2024 and ends May 31, 2025. The retainer purchases standby planning and the response undertaking described here; it does not transfer ownership of a generator, trailer, cable or accessory to Customer.

1.3 Provider may use equipment under its control or obtain compatible equipment from another supplier. Provider will determine the appropriate unit and connection arrangement after considering the load information supplied by Customer. Nothing in this Agreement identifies a particular serial-numbered unit as exclusively dedicated to Customer.

1.4 Unless the parties sign a separate purchase or installation order, Provider has no obligation to install a permanent generator at the Service Site before an emergency. Customer acknowledges that refrigeration will remain dependent on utility service until mobile generation is connected.

## 2. Mobilization notice and communications

2.1 Customer may issue a mobilization notice by the recorded dispatch telephone line after a utility interruption has lasted more than four continuous hours. The notice must identify the Service Site, the time at which the interruption began, the person available at the site and any known access or electrical hazard.

2.2 Provider will create a dispatch entry recording receipt of a qualifying notice, the site, the reporting contact and the expected transport plan. A request for information before the four-hour threshold is not by itself a qualifying mobilization notice. Provider may begin preparatory work before the threshold without waiving it for a later event.

2.3 The reporting person and dispatcher will promptly share known hazards. Estimated times supplied before route, crew and connection arrangements are confirmed are estimates. An estimate does not amend the express response undertaking in section 3.

2.4 Operational notices under this section may be given by telephone. If a follow-up message is sent by email, the sender should identify the dispatch event or Service Site so it can be associated with the original call. A delay in associating a message with an event does not itself determine whether either party has performed its contractual obligations.

## 3. Performance and delay

3.1 Following a qualifying notice, Provider shall furnish temporary generation capable of supporting Customer's refrigeration system and complete hookup at the facility within twelve hours, subject to section 3.2.

3.2 Provider shall not be responsible for delay caused by severe weather, road closure, or other conditions beyond its reasonable control. Provider shall nevertheless use commercially reasonable efforts to minimize any such delay, including consideration of available alternate equipment or transport routes when practicable.

3.3 Nothing in this Agreement requires Provider to transport equipment in violation of a governmental closure order or place personnel in unreasonable danger. When an obstruction ceases, Provider shall resume performance with commercially reasonable dispatch.

3.4 Customer is responsible for safe site access, lawful fuel supply after hookup and the accuracy of facility power requirements. Customer will identify a person with authority to admit the crew and coordinate a safe electrical connection. Provider's personnel may decline a connection that would expose persons or equipment to an unreasonable electrical hazard, pending measures necessary to address that hazard.

3.5 Dispatch estimates, resource discussions and emergency operational arrangements do not modify sections 3.1 through 3.4 unless the parties execute a written amendment under section 7.2. Neither party's acceptance of an estimate is an advance adjudication of whether a delay is excused or whether the required efforts have been made.

## 4. Service records

4.1 Provider will preserve telephone logs, dispatch logs and communications regarding declared emergency requests for not less than two years. Records may be maintained in electronic form. Customer will preserve its own information concerning the outage and the inventory affected.

4.2 A dispatch entry records information available to the person making that entry. Resource classifications such as available, reserved or shop-ready are operational notations. The legal effect of any notation, including its relationship to a binding commitment to another customer, is not prescribed by this Agreement.

4.3 On a reasonable written request concerning a disputed response, the parties will identify relevant records in their possession. This section does not waive a privilege or require disclosure in a manner inconsistent with law. It does not expand either party's obligations to create records that the Agreement does not otherwise require.

## 5. Charges outside the retainer

5.1 Actual third-party fuel expense and specified extra-rigging charges require reasonable documentation. Provider will identify the nature of an additional charge and the service event to which it relates. A disputed additional invoice does not change the monthly standby retainer unless the parties agree in writing.

5.2 Provider retains title to supplied equipment. Customer will not remove, relocate or permit unauthorized work on mobile equipment without Provider's consent, except to address an immediate threat to personal safety. The parties will coordinate removal after temporary service is no longer required.

## 6. Claims and liability

6.1 Either party may seek remedies for proven breach subject to applicable law. Neither party guarantees against losses solely resulting from the utility's grid failure. Either party may contest whether an alleged breach caused a claimed loss.

6.2 A party presenting a claim will identify the contractual provision relied upon, the conduct said to constitute breach, the loss claimed and the records reasonably available to support the claim. A loss estimate or accounting entry is not agreed to be conclusive proof of recoverable damages.

6.3 No contractual cap or liquidated-damages schedule applies to the service undertaking in this Agreement. The absence of a cap is not an admission that a particular item of loss is recoverable. The parties retain their positions concerning causation, mitigation and the nature of compensable loss.

6.4 The parties will attempt to resolve an operational claim through their designated managers before litigation, without preventing a timely filing necessary to preserve a claim or defense. This provision does not impose binding arbitration or a condition requiring the parties to agree on disputed facts.

## 7. Entire agreement and administration

7.1 This writing and Schedule A contain the parties' agreement concerning the covered Service Site. A purchase order or operational email does not add inconsistent terms merely because it is used to process an invoice or coordinate delivery.

7.2 Changes require a writing signed by both parties. Conduct undertaken during an emergency does not by itself amend the express response standard. A failure to insist upon immediate performance of one obligation is not an agreement to eliminate that obligation for future events.

7.3 Each signatory represents that the signatory is authorized to execute this Agreement for the identified party. This Agreement may be signed in counterparts. A reproduced signed counterpart may be used as evidence of execution.
