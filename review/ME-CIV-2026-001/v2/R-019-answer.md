# ANSWER AND AFFIRMATIVE DEFENSES

STATE OF MAINE · SUPERIOR COURT · CUMBERLAND, ss. · CV-2025-418

Harborlight Cold Storage, LLC v. Penobscot Power Systems, Inc.

Defendant answers the corresponding numbered paragraphs of the complaint as follows. The agreement speaks for itself; admissions concerning its existence or words do not admit Plaintiff's legal interpretation.

1. Defendant admits that Plaintiff operates refrigerated storage near Ellsworth. Defendant lacks knowledge sufficient to admit each characterization of Plaintiff's customer business and on that basis denies the remainder.

2. Admitted as to corporate status and the general nature of Defendant's business. The extent of the undertaking is governed by the writing.

3. Admitted that the complaint purports to seek contract damages from Defendant and does not allege that Defendant caused the grid failure. Defendant denies liability.

4. Admitted that the parties executed the identified written agreement.

5. Admitted as to the annual and monthly figures. Defendant does not assert nonpayment of the relevant retainer as the cause of the response at issue. Defendant denies that payment establishes the alleged breach.

6. Admitted.

7. Admitted for purposes of this answer as to the reported interruption and the presence of severe weather. Defendant lacks independent knowledge of the precise utility failure mechanism.

8. Admitted that a qualifying notice was recorded at approximately 09:55 on November 14, 2024.

9. Admitted as to the existence of the undertaking and its express qualification. Defendant denies any construction that omits section 3.2.

10. Admitted that the provision contains the identified language. The scope and legal effect of the provision are disputed.

11. Admitted as to the safety provision and Plaintiff's characterization of the duty it alleges. Defendant preserves its contention that safety and legal restrictions bear on practicability.

12. Admitted that the site was energized at approximately 07:20 on November 16, 2024. Defendant denies that this time, standing alone, establishes breach.

13. Denied insofar as it alleges a failure of the contractual undertaking. Defendant does not admit that the contemplated resource or route was practicable for the needed shipment.

14. Defendant lacks knowledge sufficient to admit every allegation concerning Plaintiff's performance and denies any allegation that Plaintiff's conduct establishes Defendant's liability. Admitted that the claim is disputed.

15. Defendant incorporates its responses to the referenced paragraphs.

16. Denied as to breach. The written terms govern the extent of Defendant's duty.

17. Denied as to causation and recoverable amount. Defendant admits that the stated amounts add to Plaintiff's initial demand, not that they are properly measured, caused or owed.

18. Defendant admits that Plaintiff describes its requested theory of recovery and denies entitlement to relief under that theory on the facts and law applicable to this action.

## Affirmative defenses

First: performance was excused or qualified to the extent provided by the severe-weather, road-closure and beyond-control provisions of the agreement.

Second: any obligation concerning reasonable efforts was limited by actual practicability, lawful transport, personnel safety and the scope of resources available under the agreement.

Third: Plaintiff must establish a causal connection between a proven breach and compensable loss. Defendant disputes that the demanded inventory and expense amounts meet that requirement.

Fourth: any recoverable amount is subject to applicable mitigation principles. This defense does not admit that a particular alternative supplier or customer action was available during the storm.

Defendant requests judgment in its favor, dismissal of the complaint's demand for relief, allowable costs and such other relief as is appropriate. Defendant does not assert a counterclaim in this answer.
