# REPLY BRIEF OF APPELLANT

MAINE SUPREME JUDICIAL COURT · LAW COURT · ME-LC-2026-147

Harborlight Cold Storage, LLC v. Penobscot Power Systems, Inc.

October 6, 2026 · Caroline Ames, Counsel for Appellant

## I. The response no longer defends the ruling actually made in categorical terms

Appellee states that its position does not delete the reasonable-efforts sentence and that the trial court's terminology may have been too broad. [R-015 §I]. Those qualifications matter. The judgment rests on the conclusion that the sentence is an operational aspiration and that the later efforts dispute is therefore immaterial. [R-013 §II]. A defense framed around the reasonableness of actual choices is not the same ruling.

Harborlight agrees that the efforts standard has limits. The opening brief discusses practicability, competing reservations and safety. [R-014 §§I–II]. The existence of limits does not make the undertaking nonactionable. Appellee's response supplies reasons it may ultimately prevail under an enforceable obligation; it does not establish that the trial court was correct to remove that obligation from the liability inquiry.

## II. The difference between a possible inquiry and certain delivery remains important

Appellee correctly identifies information missing from the submitted record. Harborlight does not cure those gaps by inventing a carrier, a vehicle weight or a route certificate. The contention is that the evidence permits examination of the efforts actually made, including the response to a conditional opening and identified resource. Whether further commercially reasonable inquiry would have produced useful action is not answered by calling the route unconfirmed.

There is a real difference between an unconfirmed route because investigation revealed an obstacle and an unconfirmed route because investigation was deferred. Sutherland's affidavit and the log do not resolve every inference concerning that difference. [R-006 §§6–10; R-007]. Harborlight does not ask the Law Court to choose the inference in its favor as a factual finding. It challenges the legal ruling that made the choice immaterial.

Appellee also observes that a provisional reservation may encumber a machine before delivery. Harborlight agrees. The record equally does not say that every provisional reservation is irrevocable, that no substitution is possible or that this unit was lawfully deliverable to Harborlight. Neither party may add those propositions to the log. The point is to evaluate the actual evidence under the correct contractual standard.

## III. The requested remand preserves the contested issues

The opening brief seeks no finding that every discarded item was lost because of delay. Appellee's causation discussion does not identify a trial-court decision on that separate ground; it acknowledges that the lower court did not reach it. The judgment should not be sustained by silently converting an unresolved damages record into a completed causation adjudication.

Harborlight does not oppose a mandate that leaves causation, the measure of loss, mitigation and actual reasonable efforts for further proceedings. That is the relief it requested. It opposes language treating the bulletin as either a conclusive clearance or a conclusive prohibition, and language treating uncertainty as a universal defense to an undertaking that expressly calls for efforts during an external delay.

## CONCLUSION

Vacate and remand. Preserve the parties' factual and remedial positions under the contractual interpretation the Law Court adopts. Do not enter judgment for Harborlight or determine a damages amount.
