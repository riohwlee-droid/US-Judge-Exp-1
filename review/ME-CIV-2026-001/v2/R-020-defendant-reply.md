# DEFENDANT'S REPLY IN SUPPORT OF SUMMARY JUDGMENT

CV-2025-418 · March 16, 2026

The opposition confirms that Plaintiff's theory is a failure of reasonable efforts after the initial obstacle rather than responsibility for the utility interruption. Defendant responds to that theory without withdrawing the contractual exclusion asserted in its motion.

## Contract and practicability

Plaintiff treats the examples of alternate equipment and transport as if their mention supplies evidence that an alternative was available. The examples identify the subjects of reasonable consideration. They do not fix a priority among customers, assign a crew or certify a lawful movement. The word practicable and the safety restriction must operate in the same reading as the reasonable-efforts direction. [R-003 §§1.3,3.2–3.3].

Plaintiff is correct that Defendant's extract does not establish every employee act. Defendant has not asked for a finding that no employee communicated with anyone during the blank intervals. The narrower submission is that a conditional route notice, a provisional resource and a deferred-review communication do not establish the operational proposition needed for Plaintiff's proposed earlier response. [R-006 §§4–10; R-007; R-008].

The parties' separate factual responses identify what is admitted and what remains contested. [R-011 §D]. Defendant requests that the Court distinguish an admission that a document contains an entry from an admission of every inference that Plaintiff attaches to that entry.

## Causation

Defendant does not suggest Frost has sworn that earlier response could never have helped. She expressly limits her knowledge. Those limitations are nevertheless relevant because the asserted loss cannot be inferred solely from the amount demanded. Bell identifies the initial claim, not an expert avoided-loss calculation. [R-004 §§13–16; R-005 §§6–8].

If the Court resolves the motion solely on interpretation, Defendant requests that the ruling say so. If the Court reaches a separate causation ground, the ground should likewise be identified. Defendant seeks judgment on Count I; it does not seek a factual narrative beyond the materials submitted.

## Conclusion

Grant the motion. Alternatively, any denial should leave Defendant free to contest actual reasonable efforts, practicability, causal allocation and the measure of loss in subsequent proceedings.
