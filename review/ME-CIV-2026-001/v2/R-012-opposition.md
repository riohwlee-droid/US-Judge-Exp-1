# PLAINTIFF'S MEMORANDUM IN OPPOSITION TO SUMMARY JUDGMENT

CV-2025-418 · Filed March 5, 2026

Harborlight opposes the motion. It does not seek judgment in its own favor, an instruction requiring unlawful transport or a finding that every item in its loss summary was caused by Penobscot. It seeks a trial under the undertaking the parties wrote, including the reasonable-efforts language that remains after an external obstacle occurs.

## I. The motion depends on treating a mandatory sentence as legally ineffective

The relevant sentence begins with an obligation that applies despite the preceding excuse. It identifies alternate equipment and transport routes as examples of the measures to be considered. [R-003 §3.2]. There is a difference between excusing the consequences of a road closure and excusing every operational choice made after a closure is reported. Harborlight's claim concerns that difference.

The agreement's safety restriction does not remove that distinction. Harborlight accepts that an unsafe movement could be refused. The question is whether Provider made the commercially reasonable efforts the writing required, including efforts to determine what was practicable. A party cannot establish the reasonableness of every decision simply by observing that some information remained unconfirmed.

## II. The evidence permits competing inferences about further inquiry

The Belfast resource entry, conditional bulletin and next-day review message do not individually prove that a truck could have reached the site that evening. Harborlight has not described them that way in its separate statement. Taken together, however, they permit inquiry into why the potentially relevant resource and route were not further verified sooner. [R-006 §§5–9; R-007; R-008].

Sutherland distinguishes equipment readiness from deployability. That distinction may be important at trial. It does not establish that no reasonable step could have been taken between the conditional opening and the next afternoon's route check. His affidavit identifies a belief concerning labor difficulty without attaching a carrier refusal, and it limits the timing of his personal route confirmation. The Court need not disbelieve him to recognize that those statements leave room for more than one supported assessment of the efforts made.

Harborlight's opposing statement also avoids overstating the log. The absence of a carrier inquiry in the submitted extract is not proof that no employee did anything. It is part of the evidence to be considered with the communications and Sutherland's own description. [R-011 C.7, D.7]. A trial is the appropriate setting to resolve a material competing inference that cannot be disposed of by the text alone.

## III. The initial loss material is limited but does not justify the requested contractual ruling

The summary is not an expert spoilage study. Frost expressly identifies the limits of her knowledge, and Harborlight does not ask the Court to convert book value into an award. [R-004 §§11–16; R-009]. Those limits do not establish that the reasonable-efforts sentence has no legal effect.

The Court should distinguish a determination about the meaning of the undertaking from the eventual proof of loss. If it reaches causation as an independent ground, Harborlight requests that the ground and the evidentiary basis be stated expressly, rather than treating the imperfection of the initial demand as a reason to eliminate a contractual undertaking.

The applicable procedural standard is M.R. Civ. P. 56(c), with the parties' facts presented under Rule 56(h). The cited official decisions concerning contractual ambiguity are Keep v. Indorf, 2024 ME 14, paragraphs 21–22, and Dahlem v. City of Saco, 2024 ME 32, paragraph 22. Neither concerns a standby-generator agreement; Harborlight invokes the interpretive framework and applies it to this writing.

## IV. Relief requested

Deny Defendant's motion. Harborlight does not request a determination that the agreement is necessarily unambiguous in its own favor. If both proposed readings are reasonably available, Defendant's reading alone cannot supply the categorical disposition requested. Remaining proof of breach, causation, loss and mitigation should proceed under the contractual standard the Court determines applicable.
