# AFFIDAVIT OF LEILA FROST

STATE OF MAINE · SUPERIOR COURT · CUMBERLAND, ss.

Harborlight Cold Storage, LLC v. Penobscot Power Systems, Inc. · CV-2025-418

I, Leila Frost, being duly sworn, state the following on personal knowledge, except where the source of information is identified.

1. I am the operations manager of Harborlight Cold Storage, LLC. I manage day-to-day access to the Ellsworth storage facility, receive refrigeration alarms, coordinate operational vendors and supervise inventory movement. I am not a refrigeration engineer, food-safety scientist or road-transport specialist.

2. I have held that position throughout the period relevant to this affidavit. In ordinary operations, I use the facility alarm panel, the operations log and customer inventory records. I can identify entries I made and communications I sent or received. I do not claim personal knowledge of entries made by Penobscot employees merely because those entries were later produced in this case.

3. On November 14, 2024, the facility's utility feed failed at 05:40. I observed the alarm-panel clock and the loss-of-utility indication. Our storage system depended on utility power. Penobscot had not installed a permanent generator at our facility under the May 17 agreement.

4. I understood that the standby agreement required us to wait until an interruption had continued for more than four hours before issuing a qualifying mobilization notice. I called the Provider dispatch line at 09:55. I identified the facility and requested temporary generation. I did not tell the dispatcher that Penobscot had caused the utility outage.

5. I had responsibility for arranging gate access and an operations contact. I remained available to coordinate admission of a crew. I did not receive a request that afternoon identifying a particular change to our gate arrangements as necessary to bring a unit to the site. That is a statement about requests communicated to me; I cannot describe the condition of every road approaching the facility.

6. I knew a serious storm was affecting the region. I saw weather conditions at our facility. I did not personally drive the commercial detour discussed in the parties' papers, measure a bridge clearance, weigh a transport rig or obtain permission for a particular generator shipment.

7. During the afternoon I sought information about possible alternatives. At approximately 17:05 I called for an update. The representative told me, in substance, that resources would be reevaluated the following day. I cannot identify from memory every word used. My account of the call is not a verbatim transcript.

8. At 17:12 I sent the follow-up email reproduced at [R-008 §A]. I asked whether the Belfast backup unit or another route could be used that night and asked to be called if access information was needed. The message was a request for information and action; I had not verified the availability of a truck, driver or generator at Belfast.

9. I received the Provider response reproduced at [R-008 §B], bearing the time 18:02. It did not give me a confirmed arrival time. I did not understand it to mean that a particular rig had been tested against a particular route restriction. The content of that message is the best evidence of what was communicated in writing.

10. I had not retained a second standby-generator provider for Harborlight. The existence of the Penobscot agreement did not cause me to acquire knowledge of the equipment or crews available to every other vendor during the storm. I do not offer an opinion that another vendor definitely could have supplied us.

11. The temperature monitor crossed our company target at approximately 23:30 on November 14. I observed the recorded reading in the operations system. A target excursion is not, by itself, my determination that all inventory was unmerchantable at that instant. The different products and their conditions were not all evaluated by me personally at 23:30.

12. Temporary generation was connected and operating at approximately 07:20 on November 16. I was the customer contact who acknowledged energization. Utility service was restored at approximately 13:10 on November 17 according to our site log. The mobile unit's arrival and the utility's restoration were separate events.

13. Harborlight subsequently discarded inventory with a recorded book value of $91,400. I reviewed the operational loss summary used in the claim. Book value is the accounting figure reported to me; it is not my opinion concerning market value or the legal measure of damages.

14. I cannot personally establish the exact time each discarded item became unmerchantable. I cannot say that energization at any particular earlier hour would have preserved every item. My inability to give those opinions should not be read as an agreement that earlier generation would have had no effect.

15. Our initial claim also included approximately $12,000 for emergency removal and disposal. Some expense could have arisen even if backup generation had arrived earlier. I do not claim that every dollar in that category necessarily resulted from the conduct alleged in the complaint.

16. More detailed invoices and accounting entries remain in Harborlight's business records. This affidavit does not attach all of them. The summary provided for this motion is reproduced separately in [R-009]. No expert causation report is attached to this affidavit.

17. I recognize the service agreement at [R-003] as the agreement used for our standby arrangement. I am not the member who signed it and do not testify to private negotiations I did not attend. Morgan Bell addresses execution and the payment records in [R-005].

18. I have separated what I observed from what I later learned through discovery. I do not ask the Court to accept a claim about the Provider's internal reservation merely on my say-so. My communications, alarm observations and operational role are the basis of the testimony I give here.

I have read this affidavit and swear that the statements based on my personal knowledge are true to the best of that knowledge.

/s/ Leila Frost
