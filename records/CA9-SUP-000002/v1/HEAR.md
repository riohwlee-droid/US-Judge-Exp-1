UNITED STATES DISTRICT COURT — CENTRAL DISTRICT OF CALIFORNIA
Mira L. Sedgewick v. Harborline Support Services, LLC
SIM-CDCA-25-CV-081 — HEARING ON SUMMARY JUDGMENT, June 29, 2026
THE COURT: Appearances, please.
MS. ORELL: Jessa M. Orell for Ms. Sedgewick.
MR. HALWICK: Brennan T. Halwick for Harborline.
THE COURT: The motion concerns the federal overtime claim alone. I have read the papers and the identified record. I understand the employee is nonexempt and the regular rate and paid weekly totals are undisputed. We will discuss whether there is evidence of work beyond those totals and whether the employer knew or should have known about it. I am not hearing live testimony today. Counsel's argument is not additional evidence.

<!-- PAGE 2 -->
THE COURT: Mr. Halwick, assume for present purposes that the plaintiff remembers actually completing an assigned task after clocking out. Is your first position that such work is not compensable?
MR. HALWICK: No, Your Honor. The tasks are assigned functions. The question is what the evidence permits a factfinder to find concerning unpaid time in the four weeks.
THE COURT: If she performed a save and a status change after the punch, what additional evidence of duration would be required?
MR. HALWICK: An estimate grounded in actual activity, not simply the later event time. She does not claim the entire interval.
THE COURT: Is it necessary to prove the exact number of minutes?
MR. HALWICK: No. We say there must be a reasonable basis to estimate some amount.

<!-- PAGE 3 -->
THE COURT: Ms. Orell, which portions of the record support an estimate as opposed to a conclusion that a task was performed?
MS. ORELL: Her testimony about the finishing notes, the May message describing a few minutes, the four pairs of business events, and the recorded punches. We are not treating the period until session termination as work.
THE COURT: Are you asking me to adopt eight, six, eleven and seven minutes?
MS. ORELL: No. Those are differences between the recorded punch and save events. They can be considered along with her recollection but are not themselves measured work.
THE COURT: Do you have another fixed numerical calculation?
MS. ORELL: Not one I ask the court to accept now.

<!-- PAGE 4 -->
THE COURT: The May 20 message appears to be the principal evidence of notice. Mr. Halwick, why should I treat the supervisor as unaware of any possible past unpaid time after receiving it?
MR. HALWICK: We do not dispute that it raised a possible concern. The distinction is between that concern and knowledge of actual uncompensated overtime. He responded by asking her to report the dates and actual time and told her not to work unpaid.
THE COURT: He could have sought more details under his policy?
MR. HALWICK: He did request details in his response. There is no specific request or reply with those details.
THE COURT: Did he notify payroll?
MR. HALWICK: He does not recall doing so.

<!-- PAGE 5 -->
THE COURT: Ms. Orell, you rely on the employer's ability to request records. Are you contending every employer must compare every application transaction against payroll?
MS. ORELL: No. This is a focused concern brought to the particular manager. The policy permits further inquiry, and the custodian said an account and date range can be used without already knowing every case number.
THE COURT: Would the report prove an amount of work?
MS. ORELL: Not by itself. It could identify dates to discuss with the employee. That is relevant to whether reasonable diligence could reveal uncompensated work.
THE COURT: Do you contend the supervisor actually reviewed these entries at the time?
MS. ORELL: We do not have proof of that.

<!-- PAGE 6 -->
THE COURT: The separate callback correction seems relevant to whether the reporting method was available. What weight should it have?
MR. HALWICK: It is a concrete example involving this employee. It shows that a small amount outside the normal time could be corrected.
MS. ORELL: We agree it was corrected. It does not resolve the earlier work or show an adequate response to the May message. The two activities and the periods are distinct.
THE COURT: Do you contend the procedure itself was fictitious?
MS. ORELL: No. Our contention concerns what the employer knew and should have done in the actual circumstances. A real reporting mechanism cannot displace the duty to pay work known to the employer.

<!-- PAGE 7 -->
THE COURT: Let's separate the dates before the May exchange from the last date. Does plaintiff have a contemporaneous report to the supervisor identifying the earlier events before May 20?
MS. ORELL: No dated report of an individual event before then. But the later message can refer to past work. The manager understood that possibility.
THE COURT: And the fourth date?
MS. ORELL: By then the issue had been brought to his attention. Whether his response reasonably resolved the concern is disputed.
MR. HALWICK: There was no new specific report after the instruction, and the evidence does not establish that he observed that event.

<!-- PAGE 8 -->
THE COURT: I will take the matter under submission. To be clear, the record includes the selected event exhibit, the payroll material, the written policy, the exchange, and the five depositions cited in the papers. The parties have not agreed upon an amount of unpaid time. There is no claim for the separate corrected callback in the four-week period. I will decide whether the evidence permits reasonable inferences on the elements, not find a damages figure today. No additional evidentiary submissions are authorized by this hearing.
MS. ORELL: Thank you, Your Honor.
MR. HALWICK: Thank you.
REPORTER: The proceeding was concluded.
