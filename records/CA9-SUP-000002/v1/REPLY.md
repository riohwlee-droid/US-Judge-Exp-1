ARGUMENT
The employer continues to attack an estimate appellant does not propose. Appellant does not ask the Court to treat the elapsed time before each accepted event as continuous work. Rather, her testimony of performing assigned closing tasks after punching out, combined with the transaction records, permits the factfinder to infer a short period of work (2-ER-130; 2-ER-133; 2-ER-159). Anderson v. Mt. Clemens Pottery Co., 328 U.S. 680, 687–88 (1946) [[AUTH:AND:P1]] permits reasonable inference in the absence of complete time records. The employer may offer evidence about the actual tasks and extent, and the factfinder may award less time than appellant contends. At summary judgment Tolan v. Cotton, 572 U.S. 650, 656–57 (2014) [[AUTH:TOL:P1]] prohibits using the technical custodian's inability to measure duration as affirmative proof that no reasonable inference can be made from the testimony.

<!-- PAGE 3 -->
Appellee acknowledges the direct message: "Sometimes I have a few minutes of notes after the clock says out." (2-ER-155). It emphasizes the response and the unrelated correction. That is a legitimate factual argument, not a dispositive legal defense. The manager recognized the possibility of past work, did not recall a focused inquiry, and was in a position to seek additional information (2-ER-110; 2-ER-113; 2-ER-67). Forrester v. Roth's I.G.A. Foodliner, Inc., 646 F.2d 413, 414–15 (9th Cir. 1981) [[AUTH:FORE:P1]] does not require payment of an invented amount, but it does require payment for work known or reasonably knowable. The employee is not asserting that the absence of automatic monitoring establishes constructive knowledge. Cadena v. Customer Connexx LLC, 107 F.4th 902 (9th Cir. 2024) (slip op. at 30–31 & n.16) [[AUTH:CAD:P1]] illustrates why the practice must be evaluated against the actual evidence; it does not decide the reserved Forrester issue. The question is whether the direct report and later event permitted a factfinder to conclude the employer should have done more to ascertain whether assigned work continued unpaid.

CONCLUSION
The record permits competing inferences on both grounds of the judgment. Under Anderson v. Mt. Clemens Pottery Co., 328 U.S. 680, 687–88 (1946) [[AUTH:AND:P1]] the employee need not supply an exact reconstruction before trial, and under Forrester v. Roth's I.G.A. Foodliner, Inc., 646 F.2d 413, 414–15 (9th Cir. 1981) [[AUTH:FORE:P1]] a reasonable factfinder may consider what the employer learned and could reasonably discover in the circumstances. Appellant requests reversal and remand, without a determination that any specific number of minutes was worked or that liability has already been established.
