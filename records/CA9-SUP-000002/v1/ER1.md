UNITED STATES DISTRICT COURT — CENTRAL DISTRICT OF CALIFORNIA
Mira L. Sedgewick v. Harborline Support Services, LLC
SIM-CDCA-25-CV-081
ORDER ON DEFENDANT'S MOTION FOR SUMMARY JUDGMENT
August 24, 2026
Defendant's motion for summary judgment is granted. The sole claim seeks overtime compensation under the Fair Labor Standards Act for four weeks ending May 26, 2024. The court considers the record cited by both parties and draws reasonable inferences in plaintiff's favor. The court does not make a finding that plaintiff's testimony was untruthful, that the application records were fabricated, or that a failure to request a payroll correction automatically bars recovery. The ruling concerns the sufficiency of the evidence on the elements of the particular claim.

<!-- PAGE 3 -->
I. UNDISPUTED FRAMEWORK
Plaintiff worked as a nonexempt hourly representative at a regular rate of $22. The payroll records reflect forty paid hours in each disputed week. PAY 1–5. 29 U.S.C. § 207(a)(1) [[AUTH:STAT:P1]] requires overtime compensation for covered work over forty hours. The duties involved completing customer cases and documenting their status. POL 1–3; PDEP 7. Application and timekeeping records are separate. The four selected application sequences reflect accepted business transactions following displayed 17:00 punches. LOG 2–5. The later session-end entries do not measure work. The dispute is not whether the tasks are business functions in general; it is whether the evidence establishes uncompensated overtime and defendant's knowledge.

<!-- PAGE 4 -->
II. SUMMARY JUDGMENT AND THE QUANTITY OF WORK
Under Tolan v. Cotton, 572 U.S. 650, 656–57 (2014) [[AUTH:TOL:P1]], the court must credit the nonmovant's evidence and draw justifiable inferences in her favor. Plaintiff's recollection of completing assigned notes may support an inference that some activity occurred after a punch. Yet the timestamps are not measurements of active work. The custodian explains that the server records accepted events and that composition, transmission and acceptance are distinct. IDEP 3–10, 16–20. Plaintiff does not recall a reliable duration and expressly disclaims equating the elapsed intervals with work. PDEP 8–13, 16, 22, 26. The coworker's general observations do not establish dates or punches. WDEP 4, 12. The court concludes that the proffered intervals lack a sufficient factual basis to infer the amount and extent of uncompensated work in each particular week.

<!-- PAGE 5 -->
III. INADEQUATE RECORDS
Anderson v. Mt. Clemens Pottery Co., 328 U.S. 680, 687–88 (1946) [[AUTH:AND:P1]] does not penalize an employee who performed uncompensated work merely because the employer's inadequate records prevent precise calculation. The employee must still produce evidence permitting a just and reasonable inference of the amount and extent of that work. The court does not hold that minutes must be proved precisely. Rather, plaintiff identifies possible upper boundaries based on event timing without a grounded estimate of active work within those boundaries. The evidence of the general workflow does not show how much of an individual operation was performed outside the paid time. On the particular showing here, the court concludes the initial evidentiary burden has not been met. In any event the knowledge element provides an independent ground.

<!-- PAGE 6 -->
IV. EMPLOYER KNOWLEDGE
Forrester v. Roth's I.G.A. Foodliner, Inc., 646 F.2d 413, 414–15 (9th Cir. 1981) [[AUTH:FORE:P1]] requires actual or constructive knowledge; an employer cannot ignore known overtime because an employee did not make a formal claim. The team reminder of May 13 emphasized completion of records and contemplated paid time or reassignment, not an instruction to work off the clock. MSG 1. The ordinary dashboard did not compare business transactions to punches. SDEP 6–11; IDEP 11–14. Plaintiff has not identified a particular event the supervisor observed after a known out punch. The May 20 message described a general possibility of missed time but no specific date or interval. The supervisor recognized that it might concern past work and responded the next morning with instructions to remain clocked in and supply information about missed time. MSG 2–3; SDEP 16–20.

<!-- PAGE 7 -->
V. CORRECTION PROCESS IN CONTEXT
The court credits plaintiff's account that the message concerned work she believed she had already performed. It also recognizes that a focused request to the technical department might identify candidate dates for further inquiry. The supervisor did not recall taking that step or following up separately. SDEP 19–21. But the contemporaneous response sought the information needed for a correction and offered assistance. On this record the court concludes that reasonable diligence did not require a historical cross-system search in the absence of further information from plaintiff. Cadena v. Customer Connexx LLC, 107 F.4th 902 (9th Cir. 2024) (slip op. at 30–31 & n.16) [[AUTH:CAD:P1]] involved a disputed policy concerning the availability of adjustments for the activities at issue. The evidence here shows a written reporting procedure, a specific invitation to report, and an actual twelve-minute correction for a separate May 30 callback. PAY 6. The court relies on those circumstances as relevant to knowledge, not on a categorical rule requiring a denied request.

<!-- PAGE 8 -->
VI. DISPOSITION
The court concludes the evidence is insufficient to create a genuine issue on the amount and extent of compensable work beyond forty hours and, independently, on actual or constructive knowledge of the alleged unpaid overtime. The ruling extends to the final disputed date after the exchange because plaintiff supplied no new information identifying actual missed work and the evidence does not establish contemporaneous observation of that event. The court has not fixed the amount of damages or determined the truth of disputed witness recollections. Judgment shall be entered for defendant on the sole claim. The separate judgment will resolve the action. Any collateral matter not decided here is governed by applicable procedure.
IT IS SO ORDERED. /s/ Leona V. March, United States District Judge.
