DEPOSITION OF TOMAS N. EWAN — January 21, 2026
Gavin M. Lorne reporting. Orell and Halwick appearing.
Q (Orell). Describe the responsibilities relevant to Exhibit LOG.
A. I maintain the customer support application's data services and assist with authenticated exports from retained application event records. I do not administer payroll and I did not supervise Ms. Sedgewick.
Q. Did you prepare the export in this litigation?
A. I produced the selected application events on January 9, 2026, from the retained tables. The requested selection concerned an identified representative account and four specified dates.
Q. Was it a live observation of her activities?
A. No. It is a historical data extract. I can explain the fields, not tell you what someone was thinking at a workstation.

<!-- PAGE 2 -->
Q. How did you identify the employee account?
A. The request supplied the employee identifier and the corresponding application account, MS214. I checked the account mapping against the retained identity information for the period. The application records are associated with the account, not a video of the human using it.
Q. What does a user-associated event mean?
A. It means the application associated the request with the authenticated user account. That association is meaningful. It is not a biometric measurement establishing which person was physically touching a keyboard at every instant.
Q. Did you find evidence of another person using the account on the four dates?
A. I was not asked to perform a forensic investigation of account sharing. I found no such evidence in the selected entries.

<!-- PAGE 3 -->
Q. How is the time in the export expressed?
A. The service stores timestamps in UTC. For the exhibit, we converted the relevant entries to Pacific local time. April and May in Southern California are daylight saving time. The displayed entries correspond to local time, not raw UTC.
Q. Was payroll time also displayed in local time?
A. The payroll exhibit uses local time as far as I understand it. I did not independently extract or audit payroll.
Q. If you compare an event at 17:08 with a punch at 17:00, does the eight-minute difference measure work duration?
A. No. It measures elapsed time between the recorded punch time and the recorded application event, assuming the clocks and interpretation are correct.

<!-- PAGE 4 -->
Q. What clock determines the timestamp for a NOTE_SAVE event?
A. The server records the accepted event time. It is not a client-side diary of each keystroke. There may be a difference between composing text, requesting a save, transmission and acceptance.
Q. Is NOTE_SAVE a mere indication that the application was open?
A. No. It indicates an accepted note save associated with the account.
Q. Does that establish that the text was typed after the payroll punch?
A. No. Some or all could have been composed before it. The event does not preserve a complete reconstruction of the editing interval.
Q. Does the event identify the business operation performed?
A. It identifies the note save operation for the case identifier listed.

<!-- PAGE 5 -->
Q. What is STATUS_CHANGE?
A. A recorded change in the status of the customer case. In the selected entries the resulting status is the one shown in the exhibit. It is a distinct accepted operation from NOTE_SAVE.
Q. Are the two event types necessarily generated by the same transaction?
A. They can occur close together in a workflow, but the table records them separately. The sequence here contains a save and a later status change. I cannot infer the entire interaction sequence from those two rows alone.
Q. Could a person leave a note written before a punch and press save later?
A. That is technically possible. The record would show the accepted save at the later time.

<!-- PAGE 6 -->
Q. What is SESSION_END?
A. It records termination of the application session. A session may end automatically because of inactivity or system behavior. A session-end entry is not a positive indication that someone was working until the displayed time.
Q. Does the table log idle time between two selected events?
A. No. There is no reliable interval measurement in these rows.
Q. If a session ends seventeen minutes after the out punch, can I say the employee worked seventeen minutes?
A. No. That would misuse the entry.
Q. Does that same limitation make a NOTE_SAVE and a STATUS_CHANGE worthless as evidence of application activity?
A. No. They describe different operations. Each field must be interpreted according to its function.

<!-- PAGE 7 -->
Q. Look at May 2 in your exhibit. What is the sequence?
A. The displayed note save is at 17:08, with a status change at 17:09 and the session ending at 17:17. Those are the selected rows for the account and case.
Q. Was the note save generated at 17:00 with eight minutes of latency?
A. The selected table does not show the initial request timestamp or a latency measurement that would establish that. I cannot make that finding.
Q. Do you have positive evidence of an eight-minute transmission delay on that date?
A. No such measurement appears in the export. I mention possible delay to explain what an accepted event timestamp does and does not establish.

<!-- PAGE 8 -->
Q. What about May 9?
A. A note save at 17:06, followed by a status change at 17:07, and a later session-end entry at 17:21.
Q. Does the fact that the first two operations appear in succession suggest that more than a session simply remained open?
A. Yes. There are accepted business operations, not merely a session ending. But the table does not tell us the precise amount of human work involved or whether the underlying text was prepared earlier.
Q. Have you identified an automatic batch process that produced those two events on that date?
A. No such batch process is identified in the selected records. I cannot categorically exclude every possible system behavior from this limited extract.

<!-- PAGE 9 -->
Q. Please review May 16.
A. The save entry is at 17:11. The status-change entry is at 17:12. The session ends at 17:25.
Q. Does the gap to the session-end entry tell you whether anyone remained physically present?
A. No.
Q. Is there a field recording the number of words entered after 17:00?
A. Not in this exhibit.
Q. Does the application require a person to finish writing a note before the status can change?
A. The workflow requires appropriate case information before a proper completion, but the exact steps can vary. The selected table is not sufficient to determine every user action or the timing of composition.

<!-- PAGE 10 -->
Q. And May 23?
A. A note save at 17:07, status change at 17:08, and session end at 17:23.
Q. Was this date later than the May 20 and May 21 exchange?
A. By calendar date, yes. I did not receive or participate in those messages.
Q. Could you identify that date in the event system in 2024 if requested?
A. Subject to the ordinary permissions and retention then applicable, a request could have identified the account and date and we could search for recorded events. I cannot say that a particular manager actually requested such a search.

<!-- PAGE 11 -->
Q. What information does the ordinary supervisor dashboard present?
A. Operational information about the queue: case identifiers, statuses, and counts. It helps allocate work. A supervisor can also open individual case records within his permissions.
Q. Does the ordinary dashboard provide a warning when a save follows an employee's payroll out punch?
A. Not as part of the system I am describing. The customer application does not routinely consume the payroll punch records for that purpose.
Q. Would the dashboard alone tell a manager that a particular representative had clocked out?
A. Not from a payroll integration in this application. A manager would need other information about the punch.

<!-- PAGE 12 -->
Q. Could a manager open a case record and learn when some events occurred?
A. Certain event information is available through operational views, depending on permissions. The detailed historical table is obtained through a report request. I would distinguish opening a case to see its current status from retrieving an event history.
Q. Do you have records establishing which individual cases Mr. Brice opened on the disputed dates?
A. Not in the material I produced. I cannot say whether he did or did not view any particular case.
Q. Could he request the detailed event report through ordinary internal channels?
A. Yes, an authorized manager could submit a report request. It would be reviewed and fulfilled according to the usual permissions.

<!-- PAGE 13 -->
Q. Is there a cost to generating a limited report?
A. There is staff time to identify the records, check the access request and prepare an extract. This is not equivalent to writing a new system. A request limited to one employee account and several weeks is more focused than an unrestricted company-wide search.
Q. Can you estimate the time it would have taken in May 2024?
A. Not precisely. It would depend on the queue, availability and the exact request. I do not have the technical team's contemporaneous workload for that period.
Q. Can the report be requested without knowing every case identifier?
A. A search can be scoped by account and date range. The request need not start with a complete list of case identifiers.

<!-- PAGE 14 -->
Q. Could someone filter the event report for accepted note saves after a specified clock time?
A. Yes. The report can be organized by local date and time and event type. To compare with actual payroll out punches, someone would need the punch data as a separate source.
Q. Is a fixed scheduled time of seventeen hundred always a reliable proxy for an actual out punch?
A. No. The scheduled end is not necessarily the recorded punch.
Q. Does this case contain a separate payroll exhibit showing the relevant out punches?
A. I have been shown that exhibit. I did not prepare it. In principle the two data sources can be compared if their time conventions and identities are reconciled.

Q (Halwick). Did anyone ask you in May 2024 to create the selected four-date report?
A. I do not recall such a request. The report in evidence was prepared for this litigation in January 2026.
Q. Did you search the entire history of every possible technical request made to the employer?
A. No. I reviewed the material relevant to this extract. My recollection and the documents I reviewed do not establish the universe of internal conversations or requests.
Q. Is it accurate to describe the litigation report as a contemporaneous alert delivered to the supervisor in 2024?
A. No. The underlying event rows are historical; the selected export was prepared in 2026.

<!-- PAGE 16 -->
Q. If a person typed a note before clocking out, requested a save, and the server accepted it shortly afterward, would that prove work after clocking out?
A. No. The timestamp alone would not establish when the human performed the task.
Q. Does the next status change necessarily establish that the user worked for the full minute separating the events?
A. No. The operations could have required little actual interaction, and there are limitations concerning transmission and processing time.
Q. Can you tell whether the employee was continuously occupied after 17:00?
A. No.
Q. Is it possible for a case to remain open after the representative stops working?
A. Yes. That is one reason the session and status data must be interpreted carefully.

<!-- PAGE 17 -->
Q. Does Exhibit LOG purport to include every case handled by the employee during all four weeks?
A. No. It is a selected export for the four dates requested. It does not establish the frequency of late activity on unselected dates or the employee's total work.
Q. Would it be statistically valid to assume that these four dates are representative of every day?
A. I have not performed a sampling analysis. I would not extrapolate from this selection without a proper basis.
Q. Could a different report include other dates?
A. Depending on retention and the request scope, yes. That report has not been produced as part of the selected exhibit.

<!-- PAGE 18 -->
Q. What information is absent from the rows that a person trying to calculate exact duration would want?
A. The full interaction history, reliable timing of composition or client activity, interruption information, and other facts about what the employee actually did. Even more detailed server rows would not necessarily supply all of that.
Q. Does your export show whether the user requested a payroll correction?
A. No. That information belongs to payroll.
Q. Does it show whether a manager knew that work continued after a punch?
A. No. It cannot establish a person's knowledge. The events might be information a manager could obtain, but the table itself does not prove when someone learned about them.

<!-- PAGE 19 -->
Q (Orell). Are you saying there is no value in the report for investigating a complaint about closing notes?
A. No. A focused report can identify possible dates and events for discussion. It just should not be treated as an exact measure of unpaid work.
Q. Could an employee who remembers performing a particular task use the entries to refresh her recollection of the date?
A. That is possible. I cannot determine the reliability of her recollection; the records can provide dates, event types and identifiers.
Q. If a note save and status change were manually performed after a punch, would the system associate them with the account?
A. Yes, in ordinary operation. The factual premise would have to be established from other evidence.

<!-- PAGE 20 -->
Q (Halwick). Is it a technical conclusion that the person worked eight, six, eleven and seven minutes respectively?
A. No. Those are differences between times under the proposed comparison, not measured task durations.
Q (Orell). Is it a technical conclusion that the person performed no work after 17:00 on those dates?
A. No. That cannot be inferred either.
Q. Have you identified any alteration to the selected timestamps?
A. No. I produced the selected retained records and explained the conversion.
Q. What is the limit of your certification?
A. The source, selection, fields and ordinary meaning of the data, not wage liability.
