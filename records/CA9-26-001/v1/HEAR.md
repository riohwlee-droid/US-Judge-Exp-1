UNITED STATES DISTRICT COURT
NORTHERN DISTRICT OF CALIFORNIA — OAKLAND DIVISION
Maren v. Aster Quay Logistics, Inc. — SIM-NDCA-25-001 (fictional)
TRANSCRIPT OF HEARING ON DEFENDANT'S MOTION FOR SUMMARY JUDGMENT
June 11, 2026, 10:00 a.m. Before the Honorable Rosa L. Vellum, fictional United States District Judge. Appearances: Alina V. Serrick for Plaintiff; Matthew J. Corven for Defendant. Simon E. Harwick, court reporter. No witnesses sworn; argument on submitted record.
THE CLERK: Calling Maren against Aster Quay Logistics, defendant's motion for summary judgment.
THE COURT: Good morning. I have read the motion, opposition, reply and exhibits. I want to focus on causation and the significance of the corrected audit rather than the merits of the underlying complaint about assignments. Is there any dispute for purposes of this motion that the written complaint is protected opposition and discharge is adverse?
MR. CORVEN: No, Your Honor. We accept those elements for this motion.
MS. SERRICK: Agreed.

<!-- PAGE 2 -->
THE COURT: Mr. Corven, your client began an audit before the complaint. But the common review and the extended review were distinct. Why should I treat the timing of the former as resolving the significance of the latter?
MR. CORVEN: The earlier audit establishes a legitimate operational reason for the review. We do not contend that the sixty-day request preceded the complaint. The prior coaching, the common flags and the resulting unresolved entries support the manager's assessment of recurrence.
THE COURT: Is there a policy requiring a sixty-day review whenever someone has a coaching note?
MR. CORVEN: No. The policy leaves scope to operational judgment. Discretion alone is not evidence that the judgment was exercised because of protected activity.
THE COURT: Did the customer complaint involve this plaintiff?
MR. CORVEN: No. It involved Bellford.

<!-- PAGE 3 -->
THE COURT: The corrected report says two entries involved disputed authorization. Are you asking me to find that the two shipments were in fact unauthorized?
MR. CORVEN: Not as a necessary condition to judgment. We submit that the employer could reasonably treat the absence of complete documentation after prior instruction as serious regardless of whether oral approvals occurred.
THE COURT: So for this motion I may assume that the two oral approvals occurred?
MR. CORVEN: Yes, to the extent the evidence supports that assumption in plaintiff's favor. The missing complete notes are undisputed. Our position is that the dispute about permission does not establish that retaliation caused discharge.
THE COURT: Where does the contemporaneous packet identify documentation alone as sufficient?
MR. CORVEN: In the April 30 operations response and the May 13 COO note, read together with the termination notice.

<!-- PAGE 4 -->
THE COURT: Ms. Serrick, why does the comparator evidence matter given that the record of Bellford's authorization was different?
MS. SERRICK: Both had incomplete notes. The manager accepted the later confirmation for Bellford and did not obtain a comparable inquiry for Maren. The extended review of Maren alone also created a different opportunity to discover recurring problems. We do not assert that the total counts are an apples-to-apples statistical comparison.
THE COURT: You have no sixty-day count for Bellford?
MS. SERRICK: Correct. Our point is not that he necessarily had the same number of problems; it is that the decision-maker invoked recurrence after choosing an unequal period of scrutiny. A jury could assess the selection and the discipline together.
THE COURT: Is the prior coaching itself disputed?
MS. SERRICK: Its occurrence is not disputed. Its meaning and weight are.

<!-- PAGE 5 -->
THE COURT: Your client admits that two notes were not complete. Does she admit four documentation failures?
MS. SERRICK: She does not dispute the missing fields in the four open entries. She disputes treating the authorization issues as confirmed unauthorized releases, and she disputes the inference that management would have imposed discharge for documentation alone in the absence of the complaint.
THE COURT: What evidence is there beyond temporal sequence?
MS. SERRICK: The changed audit scope, the absence of a comparable historical review, the different method of accepting confirmation, the manager's lack of a precomplaint instruction for the expanded review, and the absence of a fixed disciplinary threshold. We rely on their combined effect, not each as independently dispositive.
THE COURT: Are you asking me to decide whether the manager was correct about the underlying complaint?
MS. SERRICK: No.

<!-- PAGE 6 -->
THE COURT: Mr. Corven, what is the significance of the COO's approval?
MR. CORVEN: It is contemporaneous evidence that the corrected record and prior coaching were the basis for discharge. HR asked about comparable treatment and cautioned against using the complaint. The final decision did not adopt the preliminary count of twelve.
THE COURT: The COO did not independently interview Lorne or conduct a new audit, correct?
MR. CORVEN: Correct. A decision-maker can rely on reviewed operational recommendations without repeating the entire inquiry. The question is whether the record permits a reasonable finding of retaliatory causation.
THE COURT: Ms. Serrick?
MS. SERRICK: The approval was based on information supplied by the manager whose selection decision we challenge. It does not automatically break the causal chain. The packet itself acknowledges unresolved questions.

<!-- PAGE 7 -->
THE COURT: Do either of you seek additional discovery or a continuance to obtain another comparison?
MS. SERRICK: No. The motion should be decided on the existing record. Our argument concerns what can be inferred from the record as it stands; we are not asking the Court to assume the contents of a nonexistent sixty-day Bellford report.
MR. CORVEN: No, Your Honor. The record is sufficient to decide the motion.
THE COURT: Any additional evidentiary objection that must be resolved to reach the merits?
MR. CORVEN: No separate motion to strike is pending. Our arguments concerning the uncertain recollection go to significance.
MS. SERRICK: We ask the Court to draw permissible inferences in the nonmovant's favor and not to weigh witness credibility.

<!-- PAGE 8 -->
THE COURT: I understand the parties' positions. I will take the motion under submission. I am not announcing a ruling today. Counsel should not assume from my questions that I have made findings about the witnesses or the employer's motive. The briefing schedule is closed unless the Court requests further submissions.
MS. SERRICK: Thank you, Your Honor.
MR. CORVEN: Thank you, Your Honor.
THE CLERK: Court is adjourned.
(Proceedings concluded at 10:42 a.m.)
