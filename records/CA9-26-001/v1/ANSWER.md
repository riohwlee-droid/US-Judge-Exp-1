JURISDICTION AND ISSUE
Appellee agrees that the district court entered final judgment August 21, 2026 and that the appeal notice was filed September 3, 2026. 1-ER-1, 2-ER-186. Appellee does not dispute appellate jurisdiction. The issue is whether a reasonable jury could find that the protected complaint, rather than a preexisting operational concern, prior coaching and subsequently identified documentation defects, made the necessary difference to termination. Appellee accepts the district court's assumption that the oral permissions occurred and submits that the evidence concerning motive is insufficient even on that assumption.

SUMMARY OF ARGUMENT
Appellant mistakes possible imperfections in the investigation for sufficient evidence that her complaint made the necessary difference to the discharge. A genuine operational problem triggered the common review. The individualized extension had a documented explanation: recurrence after prior instruction. The resulting report identified actual defects and management incorporated the analyst's corrections. The comparison does not establish equivalent histories; the lead confirmations also differed in specificity. HR's inquiries and the COO's approval are evidence of the actual reason, not a categorical defense to improper recommendation. The district court considered the whole record and accepted the favorable version of the disputed conversations. Appellant's alternative account is possible in the abstract but is not a reasonable inference of but-for causation from the evidence presented.

ARGUMENT — I. THE GOVERNING STANDARDS
University of Tex. Southwestern Medical Center v. Nassar, 570 U.S. 338, 360 (2013) [[AUTH:NASSAR:P1]]. The record must permit a finding that the discharge would not have occurred absent the protected opposition. Tolan v. Cotton, 572 U.S. 650, 656–57 (2014) [[AUTH:TOLAN:P1]]. The Court credits the nonmovant's evidence and reasonable inferences without weighing testimony, but need not accept speculation or deem every disputed matter material. Kama v. Mayorkas, 107 F.4th 1054 (9th Cir. 2024), slip op. at 13 [[AUTH:KAMA:P1]]. Kama involved a federal employee and an independent noncooperation rationale. Its evidentiary analysis of pretext is relevant here, while the private-sector causation requirement is governed by Nassar. Timing may contribute to pretext and must be evaluated with independent evidence and the actual reason for the adverse action. Appellee does not contend that timing is irrelevant or that direct evidence is required. Its contention is that the evidence combined is insufficient to support the necessary causal finding.
The summary judgment standard derives from Fed. R. Civ. P. 56(a) [[AUTH:FRCP56:P1]].

II. THE INDIVIDUAL EXTENSION HAD A DOCUMENTED BASIS
The April 12 request identified the earlier coaching note and concern about recurrence. 2-ER-90. The coaching was documented months before the complaint and concerned the same requirement. 2-ER-86. A preliminary flag can justify further review without already being a confirmed violation. Kama v. Mayorkas, 107 F.4th 1054 (9th Cir. 2024), slip op. at 13 [[AUTH:KAMA:P1]]. The absence of a written precomplaint individual review plan does not refute the stated reason. The manager had an operational basis for investigating recurrence, and the independent initial review was not generated in response to the complaint. Appellant points to the unequal scope but identifies no policy prescribing equal windows, or contemporaneous evidence that the stated concern was knowingly fabricated.

<!-- PAGE 11 -->
III. THE CORRECTED FILE IS CONSISTENT WITH THE RECOMMENDATION
Eight flags were resolved and four remained open. 2-ER-92. The recommendation recognized the corrected count and disputed approval. 2-ER-166. Before the decision, the manager explained that complete notes mattered even if the conversations occurred. 2-ER-168. Reeves v. Sanderson Plumbing Products, Inc., 530 U.S. 133, 148 (2000) [[AUTH:REEVES:P1]]. Reeves addressed the sufficiency of an ADEA jury verdict and does not create a categorical rule of liability on this Title VII appeal. Disbelief of an employer's reason can be probative, but the record must supply a rational basis to disbelieve the actual explanation. No inconsistency arises merely from distinguishing disputed permission from a documentation failure and treating the latter as serious. The employer did not need the district court to find that permission never occurred. Appellant's criticism concerns the severity of discipline, not sufficient evidence that the documented concern was not honestly held.

IV. THE COMPARATOR DOES NOT ESTABLISH EQUIVALENT CIRCUMSTANCES
Bellford's warning addressed two open items and a specific reported permission confirmation. 2-ER-63, 2-ER-133. He could not say what an extended query would reveal. 2-ER-56. Common duties and manager do not establish equal histories, and Maren had a documented individualized coaching note. Kama v. Mayorkas, 107 F.4th 1054 (9th Cir. 2024), slip op. at 13 [[AUTH:KAMA:P1]]. Differences in the inquiry can be relevant to motive, but on this record they do not sufficiently undermine the actual distinction. No court is required to speculate that a longer audit would have yielded a comparable pattern. Appellee does not infer flawless compliance from the missing comparator records.

V. THE LEAD'S ACCOUNT DOES NOT ELIMINATE THE DOCUMENTATION REQUIREMENT
Lorne recalled circumstances consistent with the claimed approvals but could not definitively match the shipment numbers. 2-ER-40, 2-ER-42. She confirmed the requirement to complete the note. 2-ER-43. Tolan v. Cotton, 572 U.S. 650, 656–57 (2014) [[AUTH:TOLAN:P1]]. The court assumed both oral approvals occurred; thus the remaining question is not whether the employee is more credible than the manager about the conversations. The documentation defects remain even on the favorable assumption. The employer's interest in a verifiable record is genuine and was identified in contemporaneous communications, not solely in litigation.

VI. THE WHOLE RECORD SUPPORTS THE INDEPENDENT EXPLANATION
HR asked for comparison and correction, and the COO understood the limits of the corrected report. 2-ER-167, 2-ER-169, 2-ER-170. The order considered the selection, comparison, disputed authorization and approval together. 1-ER-6, 1-ER-7, 1-ER-8. Reeves v. Sanderson Plumbing Products, Inc., 530 U.S. 133, 148 (2000) [[AUTH:REEVES:P1]]. The review is not an automatic insulation from an improper recommendation. It is evidence of the basis actually stated and considered. University of Tex. Southwestern Medical Center v. Nassar, 570 U.S. 338, 360 (2013) [[AUTH:NASSAR:P1]]. Appellant's alternative causal account cannot rest merely on a manager's opportunity to retaliate and the possibility that a different personnel process would have produced a different outcome. The actual independent reasons are supported by contemporaneous evidence.

CONCLUSION
Appellee requests affirmance. It seeks no categorical rule that an audit preceding protected activity immunizes subsequent discipline. It asks the Court to apply the governing causation and summary judgment principles to this record. If the Court concludes a genuine material dispute exists, the proper alternative is remand for trial, not judgment establishing liability.
