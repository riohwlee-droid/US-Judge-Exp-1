UNITED STATES DISTRICT COURT
NORTHERN DISTRICT OF CALIFORNIA — OAKLAND DIVISION
Maren v. Aster Quay Logistics, Inc. | SIM-NDCA-25-001 (fictional)
DEPOSITION TRANSCRIPT
Deposition of Tessa M. Lorne
October 16, 2025, Oakland, California. Commenced 9:30 a.m.
Appearances: Alina V. Serrick for plaintiff; Matthew J. Corven for defendant. Simon E. Harwick, reporter (all fictional). The witness, having been duly sworn, testified as follows. The exhibits referred to during examination are the documents separately filed with the parties' motion papers.
EXAMINATION BY MS. SERRICK
Q. Please state your name and your employment position at the time of the events.
A. Tessa M. Lorne. I worked at the Oakland facility of Aster Quay Logistics.
Q. Will you tell us if you need to see a record to refresh your recollection?
A. Yes. I will distinguish what I remember personally from what the documents say.

<!-- PAGE 2 -->
Q. What did a shift lead do during a dispatch shift?
A. We coordinated the loading crews, helped resolve problems on the dispatch screen, and approved urgent releases when the normal procedure could not be completed electronically at the time. We were not the same as the dispatch manager.
Q. Could a coordinator authorize an exception without speaking to a lead?
A. Not under the controlled release procedure.
Q. Did you always enter the approval electronically yourself?
A. No. If I had to be on the floor, I might give an oral approval and tell the coordinator to record it. The coordinator had to complete the manual note by the end of the shift.
Q. Did a carrier waiting by itself permit release?
A. No.

<!-- PAGE 3 -->
Q. Did you know Elena Maren during the relevant period?
A. Yes. We worked many of the same shifts. I did not make her personnel decisions.
Q. Do you remember every urgent release from February or March 2024?
A. No. There were many transactions. I can remember particular circumstances but not every shipment number.
Q. Are you saying it would have been impossible for you to remember a particular exchange?
A. No. Some circumstances stand out. I do not want to attach a shipment number to a memory without being sure.
Q. Did you ever give Maren oral approval under the policy?
A. Yes, on more than one occasion. I cannot give a complete list.

<!-- PAGE 4 -->
Q. Look at the record for AQ-4182. What does it tell you?
A. The release was in the afternoon of March 7. The identity and destination checks were complete. The approval field was blank.
Q. Does the report itself establish whether you gave permission?
A. No.
Q. Do you recall a conversation at the desk on a March afternoon when the approval box was not updating?
A. I recall an exchange like that with Elena. I remember telling her she could proceed if the checks were complete and to make the manual entry.
Q. Can you independently identify that conversation as AQ-4182?
A. Not from the shipment number. The circumstances are consistent with my memory, but I cannot be certain.

<!-- PAGE 5 -->
Q. If you told a coordinator to proceed subject to the checks being complete, would that constitute approval under the policy?
A. If the checks really were complete and there was a qualifying urgent reason, that would be an oral approval. The coordinator would still need to make the complete note.
Q. Were you aware at the time that AQ-4182 had no completed manual note in the retained records?
A. I do not remember seeing that entry at the time. I learned that during the review of these records.
Q. Did you tell anyone that Elena had deliberately disobeyed you on that load?
A. No. I did not have a memory that would support that statement.
Q. Did you tell anyone that you were certain you approved that specific number?
A. No.

<!-- PAGE 6 -->
Q. What do you remember about March 20 and AQ-4311?
A. The date on the report shows a late-afternoon release. I remember a congested period when people were trying to clear carriers before shift end, and Elena asked about an approval that was pending.
Q. Did you say she could release it?
A. I remember saying something to the effect that if the checks were complete she could proceed and document it. I cannot identify the exact transaction from memory alone.
Q. Do you remember whether the identity and destination checks were complete?
A. I do not remember looking at those particular fields. The records displayed here say they were complete before release.
Q. Would you have intended to authorize release if those checks were not complete?
A. No.

<!-- PAGE 7 -->
Q. Does the manual reference M-320-09 bear your name?
A. No. The lead field is blank.
Q. Does the absence of your name show you did not give permission?
A. It shows there is no complete note naming me. It does not tell me what I said.
Q. Do you consider incomplete documentation unimportant?
A. No. We rely on those notes. If there is a later dispute, a blank makes it much harder to reconstruct the authorization.
Q. Did you consider a blank note equivalent in all circumstances to an unauthorized release?
A. No. Those are two different things under the procedure, even though both can be problems.
Q. Would you agree that the manager could regard repeated blanks as serious?
A. Yes.

<!-- PAGE 8 -->
Q. Did Holtwell speak to you during the April review?
A. He asked whether I remembered approving two shipments for Elena. He referred to shipment numbers. I told him I could not identify the specific numbers without the circumstances.
Q. Did you affirmatively deny that you gave permission?
A. No. I did not deny it. I said I was not sure about the exact numbers.
Q. Did you tell him you sometimes authorized urgent releases orally?
A. Yes.
Q. Did he show you the full event history and the manual note for both shipments at that time?
A. I do not recall seeing those pages. I remember a brief conversation, not a formal interview.
Q. Did he ask you to provide a written statement?
A. No.

<!-- PAGE 9 -->
Q. Did you speak to Nadia Bracken about these two shipments before May 17?
A. No, not that I recall.
Q. Did you know Elena had made a complaint about lead assignments when you spoke to Gavin?
A. I had heard people discussing the assignment process. I do not recall being told the contents of her HR complaint at that time.
Q. Did you take part in her discharge decision?
A. No.
Q. Did you provide the COO with a statement?
A. No.
Q. Could you tell the COO from your own knowledge what motive Gavin had when he made the recommendation?
A. No. I cannot know what another person was thinking.

EXAMINATION BY MR. CORVEN
Q. Is it possible that the exchanges you remember involved shipments other than AQ-4182 and AQ-4311?
A. Yes. That is why I have not said I am certain about those numbers.
Q. Would it be accurate for someone to say you confirmed authorization of both specific consignments without qualification?
A. No. I can say the circumstances are consistent with my recollection, not that I have positively matched them.
Q. Did the company require contemporaneous documentation to help address precisely that uncertainty?
A. Yes.
Q. If a coordinator failed to identify the lead before leaving the shift, had the coordinator fully complied with the note requirement?
A. No.

<!-- PAGE 11 -->
Q. Do you recall Bellford's customer shipment AQ-4470?
A. I was not the lead whose confirmation was reported for that shipment. I saw the customer issue discussed later, but I did not observe the approval conversation.
Q. Can you testify that his circumstances were identical to Maren's?
A. No. I did not see his exchange.
Q. Could a manager distinguish a specific confirmation from a qualified recollection like yours?
A. Yes. Those are different types of information.
Q. Can you say that the manager actually assessed the two employees impartially?
A. No. I did not make the personnel comparison. I can only describe what he asked me and what I told him.

<!-- PAGE 12 -->
FURTHER EXAMINATION BY MS. SERRICK
Q. Had someone shown you the detailed field histories and asked you about the circumstances in April, might you have recalled more?
A. Possibly. It was closer in time then. I cannot say what I would have remembered.
Q. Did you refuse to cooperate with the investigation?
A. No. I answered the questions I was asked.
Q. Did you consider Elena's explanation of oral approval implausible on its face?
A. No. We did use oral approvals for urgent releases. I could not verify both shipment numbers.
Q. Are you changing your answer about whether you denied giving permission?
A. No. I never told Gavin that I had not authorized either specific load.
