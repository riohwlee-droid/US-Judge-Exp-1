UNITED STATES DISTRICT COURT
NORTHERN DISTRICT OF CALIFORNIA — OAKLAND DIVISION
Maren v. Aster Quay Logistics, Inc. | SIM-NDCA-25-001 (fictional)
DEPOSITION TRANSCRIPT
Deposition of Jules T. Bellford
October 20, 2025, Oakland, California. Commenced 9:30 a.m.
Appearances: Alina V. Serrick for plaintiff; Matthew J. Corven for defendant. Simon E. Harwick, reporter (all fictional). The witness, having been duly sworn, testified as follows. The exhibits referred to during examination are the documents separately filed with the parties' motion papers.
EXAMINATION BY MS. SERRICK
Q. Please state your name and your employment position at the time of the events.
A. Jules T. Bellford. I worked at the Oakland facility of Aster Quay Logistics.
Q. Will you tell us if you need to see a record to refresh your recollection?
A. Yes. I will distinguish what I remember personally from what the documents say.

<!-- PAGE 2 -->
Q. Were you a dispatch coordinator in the same Oakland rotation as Maren?
A. Yes. We performed the same general kind of work. We did not always handle the same loads or work the same hours.
Q. Who was your manager?
A. Gavin Holtwell.
Q. Did the controlled release procedure apply to you?
A. Yes. It was distributed to all of us. I understood that exceptions required lead permission and a complete note.
Q. Did you have discretion to approve your own urgent releases?
A. No. We could ask a lead. We could not approve the exception ourselves.
Q. Did you receive instruction on the documentation process during ordinary staff meetings?
A. Yes, from time to time.

<!-- PAGE 3 -->
Q. Let's discuss the customer shipment AQ-4470 on April 2.
A. I remember the load and the subsequent complaint. The destination confirmation was not visible in the release screen when the carrier was ready.
Q. Why did you send the release?
A. I asked a lead about the missing confirmation. The lead told me the destination had been confirmed by telephone and to release the load. I believed the confirmation had been completed.
Q. Did you record the reason for the urgent exception?
A. The manual note had the lead name but the reason field was blank. I acknowledge that it was incomplete.
Q. Did the customer complain?
A. Yes. I learned about the complaint later that day.

<!-- PAGE 4 -->
Q. Was the destination field completed after release?
A. That is what the system record shows. I don't remember the exact minute it populated.
Q. Did you personally make the telephone call confirming the destination?
A. No. I relied on what the lead told me.
Q. Could you produce a contemporaneous record of what the lead said?
A. I don't have a recording. There is the note naming the lead, but the reason is blank.
Q. Did management tell you that the lead later confirmed the instruction?
A. Yes. Gavin said he had checked with the lead and that the documentation was still a problem.
Q. Did you see a signed statement from the lead?
A. No.

<!-- PAGE 5 -->
Q. What other entries were discussed with you?
A. There were four raw flags in the common report. Two were resolved. One other open entry had an incomplete reason field. I was given a warning concerning the two open items.
Q. Were you ever told that the four raw flags were four proven violations?
A. No. I was told two had been corrected.
Q. Did you have access to Maren's report then?
A. No. I did not have her personnel or audit documents.
Q. Did you know whether she had received oral permission for particular shipments?
A. No. I was not present at the two conversations described in her deposition.
Q. Did you participate in the decision to discharge her?
A. No.

<!-- PAGE 6 -->
Q. What corrective action did you receive on May 2?
A. A written warning.
Q. Was that a suspension?
A. No. I continued working. I had to review the policy and have my next five urgent exceptions checked.
Q. Were you warned that future violations could lead to discharge?
A. Yes.
Q. Did you receive a formal written warning for documentation before May 2?
A. Not that I recall. There had been ordinary reminders about the policy.
Q. Had you ever been told individually to complete an incomplete entry?
A. I remember a lead asking me once to fill in a reason field. I don't know whether that was recorded as coaching. I had no formal meeting with HR about it before this warning.

<!-- PAGE 7 -->
Q. Did anyone tell you your transactions had been examined back to February 4?
A. No. The report discussed with me was for March 21 through April 3.
Q. Can you say that you had no incomplete manual notes in February or early March?
A. No. I do not remember every transaction. I would have to look at the records.
Q. Can you say that a sixty-day search would find additional problems?
A. No. I do not know what it would find.
Q. Did the company ask you to provide a full account of all prior urgent releases during the sixty-day period?
A. Not as part of the April review. I answered questions about the common report.

<!-- PAGE 8 -->
Q. Were you assigned acting-lead duties in early 2024?
A. I received some acting-lead assignments. I was not told why every individual selection was made.
Q. Had you received permanent promotion to a lead position?
A. No. I remained a coordinator.
Q. Were you aware that Elena had asked for opportunities?
A. I had heard her discuss the assignment process. I did not see her email to HR then.
Q. Did you know she had made a sex-discrimination complaint when you received your warning?
A. Not specifically. I learned about the written complaint later.
Q. Did Gavin ever tell you that an employee should be punished for complaining about assignments?
A. No.

<!-- PAGE 9 -->
EXAMINATION BY MR. CORVEN
Q. Do you claim that the underlying customer issue was minor?
A. No. A customer complaint matters. I thought I had approval, but I understood why the company was concerned.
Q. Was the instruction you received from the lead specific to the load?
A. Yes, as I recall. I asked about that shipment and was told the destination had been confirmed.
Q. Does your testimony establish that Lorne gave the same kind of instruction to Maren?
A. No. I did not hear those conversations.
Q. Did your warning identify the lead in the manual note?
A. The underlying note contained the lead name. The warning addressed the missing reason and the destination issue.

<!-- PAGE 10 -->
Q. Did you understand that the documentation procedure was the same even when a lead gave oral permission?
A. Yes. The note still had to be complete.
Q. Did you have a prior documented coaching note like the one in Maren's file?
A. I am not aware of one in my file. I did receive ordinary reminders.
Q. Do you believe everyone who gets a warning must be treated identically regardless of prior history?
MS. SERRICK: Objection to the form, calls for speculation.
A. I do not set the policy. I would expect the manager to consider the circumstances.
Q. Have you seen the complete records of the other coordinators?
A. No.

<!-- PAGE 11 -->
FURTHER EXAMINATION BY MS. SERRICK
Q. Were you asked to identify the exact time and words of the lead's oral approval for AQ-4470 before receiving a warning?
A. I was asked what happened. I did not provide a word-for-word account or an exact timestamp of the conversation.
Q. Did you understand that the manager accepted your explanation that permission had been given?
A. Yes. He said the lead had confirmed it.
Q. Do you know whether the same inquiry was made for every employee?
A. No.
Q. Is your testimony that you never made a documentation mistake outside the common audit window?
A. No. I cannot make that statement.
