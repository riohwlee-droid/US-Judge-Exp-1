SENTENCING — UNITED STATES
MS. RENWICK: The government requests a sentence of six months of imprisonment and one year of supervised release. This was not an offense involving a monetary loss or missing equipment, and we are not asking the Court to punish the defendant as if it were. The monitoring process depends on accurate representations about the sources of verification. The jury found the charged answer was deliberately false and material. The government submits that the sentence should reflect the seriousness of giving false information during such a federal review and provide appropriate deterrence. The defendant has no prior convictions reflected in the criminal history calculation. We recognize his personal circumstances and the absence of a financial loss as mitigating facts. The government requests no restitution and no fine on this record, apart from the mandatory assessment. We do not ask for an enhancement based on the March certification as a separate criminal act.

THE COURT: Are there any victims requesting to be heard or any restitution claims before the Court?
MS. RENWICK: No individual has requested to address the Court and there is no restitution claim supported by the evidence. The agency's monitoring file is not a claim for money in this case.
THE COURT: I will not make a finding of an award loss absent evidence. What is the government's position on conditions of supervision?
MS. RENWICK: Ordinary mandatory and standard conditions, without a special condition concerning the cooperative's property or the defendant's professional license. SENTENCING — DEFENSE
MR. CARROW: We request probation or another noncustodial disposition. The defendant has a stable history of employment and no prior criminal convictions. He was released throughout the case and complied with his appearance obligations. The record establishes that the property was accounted for and that the monitoring question did not result in a demonstrated loss. He has experienced substantial disruption from the prosecution and conviction. He maintains that the charged answer was a reference to the documentary review, and we will seek appellate review. We do not ask the Court to set aside the verdict at sentencing. We ask the Court to consider the nature and circumstances of the offense, including the fact that the clarification was given during the same interview and the receipt was ultimately supplied. A sentence without imprisonment would adequately address the statutory purposes on these facts.

MR. CARROW: We do not dispute that accuracy in communications to an agency is important. Our submission is that the least severe sufficient sentence in this case is not a term of incarceration. There is no evidence that the defendant diverted the equipment, altered the receipt or obstructed the production of records. The government agrees that there was no restitution amount. The defendant has appeared when required and has no demonstrated need for incapacitation. His willingness to comply with supervision has been evident during the pretrial period. We request that the Court not treat his exercise of the right to trial or his continued legal challenge as aggravating factors. We recognize the verdict controls unless reversed. We ask the Court to provide the required explanation for the chosen sentence in light of the advisory range and the circumstances actually established.

COURT'S REASONS
THE COURT: I have considered the advisory range and the statutory factors, including the nature of the offense, history and characteristics of the defendant, seriousness, deterrence, protection of the public, and available sentences. The charged offense concerns a representation to an agency about a matter within its monitoring responsibility. The jury's verdict establishes guilt for sentencing purposes. I do not impose additional punishment for a separate certification offense, a theft or monetary loss, none of which is a charged or established basis here. The lack of a demonstrated loss, the fact that the instruments were accounted for, the absence of prior convictions, and the defendant's compliance on release all weigh in mitigation. I have also considered the defense's request for a noncustodial sentence and the defendant's personal statement.

<!-- PAGE 9 -->
THE COURT: Accuracy about the source of property verification is important to the exercise of the federal monitoring function. The verdict found that the defendant knowingly and willfully made the material false statement. The sentence should reflect that conduct and deter similar deliberate false representations. I conclude that a short custodial term at the top of the advisory range is sufficient but not greater than necessary on this record. I have considered the alternative of probation and conclude that it would not sufficiently reflect the seriousness and deterrence purposes in this particular case. I am not increasing the sentence because the defendant exercised his right to a trial or continues to assert his legal challenge. I do not infer additional misconduct from the mere denial of the motion for acquittal. I decline to impose a fine after considering the financial circumstances and the other obligations reflected in the report.

PRONOUNCEMENT OF SENTENCE
THE COURT: On Count One, I commit the defendant to the custody of the Bureau of Prisons for a term of six months. Following release from imprisonment, he will be on supervised release for one year, subject to the mandatory and standard conditions I will state and incorporate in the judgment. The special assessment is one hundred dollars. No fine or restitution is imposed. The Court does not order civil repayment or resolve the agency's monitoring item. The defendant shall report for surrender as directed by the United States Marshal under the separate reporting instructions. The existing conditions remain applicable until surrender unless modified by a separate order. Counsel may make any application concerning release pending appeal by separate motion; this pronouncement does not decide such an application. The written judgment will reflect the terms pronounced today.

THE COURT: During supervised release the defendant shall not commit another federal, state or local crime and shall comply with the mandatory conditions applicable by law, including lawful reporting, compliance with the supervising officer's directions and the required restrictions concerning controlled substances. He shall report to the probation office as directed after release, keep the officer informed of changes in residence and employment, seek permission for travel outside the authorized district as required, answer the officer's lawful inquiries truthfully, and permit supervision in accordance with the standard conditions set out in the judgment. The Court imposes no special employment or grant-related restriction. The mandatory assessment is due as directed. The judgment's conditions attachment will set out the full conditions consistent with the oral pronouncement. If there is a conflict, the parties shall promptly notify the Court for appropriate correction.
