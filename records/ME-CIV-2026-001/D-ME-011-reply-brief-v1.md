# Appellant's Reply Brief

**SIMULATED FILED LAW COURT REPLY.** ME-LC-2026-147. Filed Oct. 6, 2026. Table of authorities: M.R. Civ. P. 56(c), (h); Keep v. Indorf, 2024 ME 14, ¶¶21–22; Agreement §3.2.
Appellee now concedes that the second sentence of §3.2 'can guide operational decisions and bear on causation.' Appellee Br. 2. That concession demonstrates why the trial court's conclusion that the language is a nonactionable aspiration cannot justify judgment on every fact pattern.
This appeal does not rest on a requirement that workers drive through closure orders. A later conditional detour and unexplored alternate unit raise questions about what was practicable after the original barrier, and why an inquiry or dispatch was deferred until the following afternoon. A provisional reservation does not, without more, establish unavailability.
Appellee disputes causation, but the Superior Court expressly declined to reach it as an independent ground. App. 6. Plaintiff agrees it cannot win damages merely by proving late performance. Nor may the Court decide the contested factual inferences against plaintiff to affirm on unlitigated detail.
Requested disposition remains vacatur and remand, not judgment for appellant.
Signed /s/ Caroline Ames, Esq., Oct. 6, 2026; service certified.
