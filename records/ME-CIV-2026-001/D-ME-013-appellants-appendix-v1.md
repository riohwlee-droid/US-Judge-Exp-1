## Appendix p.6: Superior Court judgment (part 2)

_Source D-ME-007, source logical page 2. Reproduced as filed._

The second sentence of §3.2 instructs Provider to use 'commercially reasonable efforts to minimize' delays. The Court construes it as an operational aspiration within an express excuse, rather than an independent source of damages liability for a severe-weather delay. Otherwise the excuse would have no practical function. Read as a whole, the Agreement leaves losses from such weather delays with Customer.
Because undisputed severe weather caused the initial disruption and transport delay, the Court regards disagreement over alternative routes and backup equipment as immaterial. On this reading it need not determine whether any later failure to act was commercially reasonable. Plaintiff's damage and causation proof would independently present substantial questions, but the Court does not reach those issues as grounds for judgment.
ORDER: Defendant's motion for summary judgment is GRANTED. Judgment shall enter for Penobscot Power Systems, Inc. on the entire complaint. All relief sought by Plaintiff is DENIED. There are no remaining counts or parties. Costs may be pursued as allowed by rule.

## Appendix p.19: Plaintiff's opposition (part 3)

_Source D-ME-005, source logical page 3. Reproduced as filed._

ARGUMENT. Provider's motion asks the court to erase §3.2's second sentence or treat it as a nonjusticiable aspiration. The first sentence governs fault for conditions beyond control; the second governs commercially reasonable response when those conditions exist. Even if the latter is a dependent qualification of the former rather than an independent covenant, a factfinder could find that inaction after restricted access reopened was not commercially reasonable.
A nonmovant need not prove its eventual damages at the summary-judgment stage when the motion fails to establish no material factual issue as to liability. Nevertheless, Harborlight acknowledges that proof of market loss, time of spoilage and mitigation remains an open question. It requests denial of the motion and trial, not immediate judgment in its own favor.
Respectfully submitted, /s/ Caroline Ames, Esq. Certificate of service March 5, 2026.
