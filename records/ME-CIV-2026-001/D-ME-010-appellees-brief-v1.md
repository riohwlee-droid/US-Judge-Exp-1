# Brief of Appellee Penobscot Power Systems, Inc.

**SIMULATED FILED LAW COURT BRIEF.** Docket ME-LC-2026-147. Filed Sept. 22, 2026.
TABLE OF CONTENTS (stable logical pages): Preliminary Statement/Facts p.1; Scope and Standard p.2; Contract Reading p.3; Rule 56/Conclusion p.4.
TABLE OF AUTHORITIES: M.R. Civ. P. 56(c), (h) pp.2,4; Keep v. Indorf, 2024 ME 14, ¶¶21–22 pp.2–3; Dahlem v. City of Saco, 2024 ME 32, ¶¶16,22 pp.2–3; Agreement §§3.1–3.3 pp.1,3.
PRELIMINARY STATEMENT. Plaintiff's interpretation would subject the weather exception to an after-the-fact jury question any time a storm prevents emergency delivery. The parties allocated the risk of weather-caused delays to the Customer. The phrase 'when practicable' and §3.3's safety language are important limits. No evidence demonstrates that a truck with compatible unit and crew could have supplied plaintiff during the restricted period.
STATEMENT OF FACTS. Provider received notice at 09:55, attempted mobilization at 10:44, and confirmed dangerous conditions (App. 12–14). The available log showed a 200-kW unit provisionally promised to another customer. A partial detour bulletin warned of load restrictions and required individual route checks. The company connected power on Nov. 16, and the Customer's loss summary did not identify which inventory had already deteriorated when alternate routes reopened (App. 12–14).

SCOPE AND STANDARDS. Summary judgment and contractual ambiguity are reviewed de novo. See Dahlem, 2024 ME 32, ¶¶16,22; Keep, 2024 ME 14, ¶21. The reviewing court may affirm on a legal basis supported by the record but should not decide new disputed facts. There was no trial and no jury determination.
QUESTION PRESENTED. Whether the weather excuse defeats plaintiff's breach claim when severe conditions caused the interruption and the nonmovant offered no non-speculative proof that commercially reasonable alternate dispatch would have avoided loss.
Provider does not claim that the second sentence can never matter. It can guide operational decisions and bear on causation when feasible mitigation is supported by evidence. But a reading that compels speculative second-guessing about dispatcher choices should be rejected.


<!-- PAGE 3 -->

ARGUMENT I — THE CONTRACT WAS NOT INSURANCE AGAINST THE STORM. Section 3.1 is expressly subject to the exception. Section 3.2's first sentence protects from 'delay caused by severe weather, road closure, or other conditions beyond [Provider's] reasonable control.' Section 3.3 forbids unsafe operations and unlawful transport. The parties did not guarantee a backup generator at any risk. The Court must harmonize all three provisions; the trial court treated the mitigation language as operational conduct rather than an independent promise to pay damages whenever a storm creates obstacles.
Even if 'shall nevertheless' is mandatory in some circumstances, the ordinary meaning of 'commercially reasonable efforts' is bounded by resources, availability, legality and time. The Court should not treat a 'shop-ready' notation as proof of available labor, a lawful truck route, or an unencumbered generator.


<!-- PAGE 4 -->

ARGUMENT II — THE RECORD DOES NOT CREATE A MATERIAL ISSUE. Plaintiff offered only a bulletin allowing certain commercial vehicles to try a detour, not proof that this unit and rig met its limits. The Belfast unit was provisionally reserved, and plaintiff produced no driver or carrier willing to haul it. Frost admitted she could not determine when individual inventory became unmerchantable. No admissible materials quantified avoided loss from an earlier dispatch.
ALTERNATIVE RESPONSE. If the Law Court finds independent mitigation duties or a genuine interpretive ambiguity, it should not enter judgment for Plaintiff; an appropriate remand should address what reasonable efforts meant under the circumstances and whether their absence caused compensable loss.
CONCLUSION. Affirm judgment; alternatively remand without liability findings if the contract interpretation is altered.
