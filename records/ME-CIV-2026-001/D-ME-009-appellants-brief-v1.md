# Brief of Appellant Harborlight Cold Storage, LLC

**SIMULATED FILED LAW COURT BRIEF.** Docket ME-LC-2026-147. Filed Aug. 18, 2026. Harborlight Cold Storage, LLC v. Penobscot Power Systems, Inc.
TABLE OF CONTENTS (stable logical pages): Nature and Facts—p.1; Questions and Standard—p.2; Contract Text—p.3; Material Disputes and Relief—p.4.
TABLE OF AUTHORITIES: M.R. Civ. P. 56(c), 56(h) pp.2,4; M.R. App. P. 7A p.1; Keep v. Indorf, 2024 ME 14, ¶¶21–22 p.2; Dahlem v. City of Saco, 2024 ME 32, ¶¶16,22 p.2; contract §§3.1–3.3 pp.1–3. 
NATURE OF THE CASE. Provider expressly promised a twelve-hour mobile generator response to a qualifying notice, subject to excusing obstacles. The same provision said Provider 'shall nevertheless' use commercially reasonable efforts to minimize such a delay. The trial court made the mitigation sentence legally inconsequential and did not evaluate the disputed post-closure conduct.
STATEMENT OF FACTS. The Agreement was executed May 17, 2024 (App. 9–11; D-ME-003 pp.1–3). A power loss occurred Nov. 14, 2024 at 05:40; qualifying notice was logged 09:55 (App. 12–14; D-ME-006 pp.1–2). The first dispatch was cancelled at 10:44. An alternate detour was posted at 16:15, subject to limits; a Belfast unit was marked ready. No completed hookup occurred until 07:20 Nov. 16. (App. 12–14.) The June 3 judgment granted all relief to Provider (App. 5–6).

QUESTIONS PRESENTED.
1. Does Agreement §3.2 unambiguously transform the Provider's separate promise to use commercially reasonable efforts during an excused weather delay into a nonactionable aspiration?
2. If §3.2 preserves any enforceable mitigation obligation, were disputes concerning the available equipment, transport and timing material to Defendant's Rule 56 motion?
STANDARDS OF REVIEW. A grant of summary judgment is reviewed de novo, with the facts and supported inferences considered as Rule 56 requires; interpretation and ambiguity of a contract are questions of law for the court, reviewed de novo. See Dahlem, 2024 ME 32, ¶¶16,22; Keep, 2024 ME 14, ¶21. If a contract is ambiguous, interpretation may require factfinding. Keep, ¶22.
SUMMARY OF ARGUMENT. The trial court's categorical excuse is not the only plausible textual reading. A duty to take reasonable measures can coexist with a rule that a provider is not liable for delay caused solely by external obstacles. Reversing here would not prove liability or damages; it would require the trial court to apply the clause and Rule 56 to contested evidence.

ARGUMENT I — EACH SENTENCE HAS WORK TO DO. Section 3.1 sets a twelve-hour baseline. Section 3.2 first sentence protects Provider from fault for weather and road conditions it cannot control. The second sentence begins 'nevertheless,' signaling an obligation remaining despite that excuse. A reading of 'shall nevertheless use commercially reasonable efforts' as purely advisory would deprive the verb 'shall' and the explicit examples ('alternate equipment or transport routes') of effect. If an external road closure made all dispatch impossible through Nov. 16, Provider might be excused. But §3.2 is not naturally a blanket exemption from reviewing any conduct once severe weather occurred.
Under Maine contract-law principles, courts start with the text as a whole, avoid rendering language meaningless, and decide whether competing interpretations are reasonable. Dahlem, 2024 ME 32, ¶22; Keep, 2024 ME 14, ¶¶21–22. The trial court did not hold a factfinder hearing, and the instrument neither defines 'commercially reasonable' nor states that the mitigation sentence creates no duty. Even if the text is ambiguous rather than unambiguous in Plaintiff's favor, summary judgment is not warranted on Defendant's interpretation alone.

ARGUMENT II — THE SUMMARY-JUDGMENT EVIDENCE IS NOT CONCLUSIVE. The motion conceded the first aborted dispatch, a potential commercial detour later in the day, and a 200-kW Belfast unit provisionally reserved. An employee email said only that the team would 'review tomorrow.' Whether dispatch after the route notice was practicable depends on vehicle clearance, drivers, competing reservations and destination access. The Court could not infer against Plaintiff that those barriers necessarily persisted without resolving contested matters. Opposing Statement PASMF 1–7 (App. 17–19).
ARGUMENT III — REMEDY. Appellant does not ask for a finding that all goods spoiled because of Defendant. The affidavits acknowledge unresolved valuation and causation. Those issues belong to further proceedings if the legal duty is recognized and the defense fails on its own motion.
CONCLUSION. Vacate the judgment and remand for application of the enforceable or ambiguous mitigation obligation, subject to remaining factual and remedial questions; alternatively remand for an explanation of why §3.2's second sentence has no legal significance.
