# Defendant's Motion for Summary Judgment and Statement of Material Facts

**SIMULATED FILED MOTION.** Superior Court CV-2025-418. Filed February 12, 2026. Defendant moves for judgment on Count I under M.R. Civ. P. 56. Defendant's counsel asserts the undisputed storm and road restrictions bring this case within the specific excuse in §3.2, and plaintiff cannot establish actionable breach merely from late arrival. Plaintiff must respond under Rule 56(h) with numbered admissions, denials or qualifications and record support.
ARGUMENT I — RULE 56. There is no genuine dispute that a severe weather system shut regional routes and interrupted supply. The contractual twelve-hour undertaking is expressly subject to the weather exclusion. Reading the separate mitigation sentence as an absolute guarantee would nullify the first sentence.
ARGUMENT II — NO REASONABLE ALTERNATIVE. Witness evidence demonstrates substantial staffing and transport restrictions. The plaintiff's suggestion that a generator in Belfast could be moved earlier depends on speculation about drivers, bridge limits and equipment suitability.
ARGUMENT III — CAUSATION. Even if the mitigation sentence imposed a distinct obligation, the disputed inventory records do not establish when spoilage became irreversible. Defendant does not move for a declaration that the claimed amount is admitted.
Requested relief: judgment for defendant on the complaint.

DISCUSSION OF CLAIMED MATERIALITY
1–4 and 7–8 are accepted as core chronological and contractual facts. The dispositive issue in Provider's view is that the first sentence of §3.2 excuses any twelve-hour response during the storm. The second sentence requires sensible conduct within that exception but cannot make Provider responsible for an inability to deliver safely. Allegations about availability after 16:15 do not show a safely crewed, properly rated unit could be dispatched with power connections completed by the next morning.
The Rule 56 record includes sworn declarations and Provider's contemporaneous log. Defendant recognizes its witness is not a government road official and that a county bulletin does not identify every local route. Defendant asks that all reasonable inferences be considered, but maintains they are insufficient as a matter of law.
