# Complaint for Breach of Contract — Harborlight v. Penobscot

**SIMULATED FILED PLEADING.** Superior Court, Cumberland County, CV-2025-418. Filed August 8, 2025.
Plaintiff Harborlight Cold Storage, LLC alleges as follows:
1. Plaintiff is a Maine limited liability company operating refrigerated inventory storage at an Ellsworth facility for seafood distributors.
2. Defendant Penobscot Power Systems, Inc. is a Maine corporation providing temporary generation and emergency electrical services.
3. On May 17, 2024 the parties entered into a written Emergency Generation Support Agreement, attached to this complaint as Exhibit A and separately preserved in the record as D-ME-003.
4. Plaintiff agreed to an annual standby retainer of $45,600, payable in monthly installments of $3,800, for emergency mobilization and associated planning. Plaintiff paid all monthly amounts due through December 2024.
5. At 05:40 on November 14, 2024 the utility feed to plaintiff's facility failed during a severe coastal storm; no evidence in this complaint alleges that defendant caused the grid failure.
6. Plaintiff advised defendant by phone at 09:55 that the outage had continued more than four hours, and requested temporary generation.
7. Defendant did not provide working temporary generation at the site until approximately 07:20 on November 16, 2024.

8. Plaintiff alleges defendant could have used alternate transport or equipment sooner, including a unit held at a Belfast depot, after an alternate highway route became passable on the afternoon of November 14.
9. Contract §3.2 excuses delay caused by severe weather or road closure, but also requires commercially reasonable mitigation of such delay. Plaintiff alleges unreasonable failure to mitigate, not that the storm was fictitious.
10. Plaintiff lost refrigerated inventory and paid for extraordinary removal and disposal, in amounts to be proved at trial. Its initial demand totals $103,400, comprising an asserted $91,400 product loss and $12,000 response expense; defendant disputes both amounts and causation.
11. Plaintiff performed its own obligations, including access to site and the agreed service retainer.
COUNT I — BREACH OF CONTRACT. Defendant had a duty to use commercially reasonable efforts to minimize an excused delay and failed to do so. This failure allegedly caused recoverable loss.
RELIEF. Plaintiff requests compensatory damages in a proven amount, pre- and postjudgment interest as permitted by law, costs, and any other appropriate relief. Plaintiff requests jury trial on issues triable by jury.
