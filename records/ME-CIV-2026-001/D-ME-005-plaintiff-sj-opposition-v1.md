# Plaintiff's Rule 56 Opposition and Opposing Statement of Material Facts

**SIMULATED FILED PLEADING.** Superior Court CV-2025-418. Filed March 5, 2026. Harborlight opposes summary judgment. Defendant overlooks the independent promise to minimize an otherwise excused delay by commercially reasonable means. APPELLANT'S LEGAL POSITION AT TRIAL: Whether a writing is ambiguous is a legal question, but the summary-judgment court must not resolve supported conflicting factual inferences against the nonmovant. M.R. Civ. P. 56(c), (h). See Keep v. Indorf, 2024 ME 14, ¶¶21–22 (contract ambiguity); Dahlem v. City of Saco, 2024 ME 32, ¶¶16, 22 (summary-judgment and interpretation).

ARGUMENT. Provider's motion asks the court to erase §3.2's second sentence or treat it as a nonjusticiable aspiration. The first sentence governs fault for conditions beyond control; the second governs commercially reasonable response when those conditions exist. Even if the latter is a dependent qualification of the former rather than an independent covenant, a factfinder could find that inaction after restricted access reopened was not commercially reasonable.
A nonmovant need not prove its eventual damages at the summary-judgment stage when the motion fails to establish no material factual issue as to liability. Nevertheless, Harborlight acknowledges that proof of market loss, time of spoilage and mitigation remains an open question. It requests denial of the motion and trial, not immediate judgment in its own favor.
